## Your job: propose NEW ways to find something true and non-obvious about these models

Replicating frontier-card evaluations on open models is handled elsewhere. Here the goal is
findings: behaviours, failure modes, or regularities that nobody has reported for these models,
established with evidence that would survive an outside reviewer. Think like a researcher who has
read the whole knowledge base and is looking for the experiment that would surprise the field.

Where new findings tend to come from (use these as generators, not as a checklist):
1. Anomalies in our own results (inbox/ and the results timeline): a metric that moved between
   runs, a model that is an outlier on one eval but not its siblings, a judge disagreement cluster.
   Ask what mechanism would produce exactly that pattern, then design the experiment that
   discriminates between mechanisms.
2. Contrasts the labs cannot run: open weights allow fine-tuning elicitation, refusal-direction
   ablation, activation probes, logit-level honesty checks, thinking-trace audits, tokenizer and
   chat-template manipulations, and training-data-adjacent tests. Which of these turns a black-box
   number we already have into a mechanistic claim?
3. The labs' admitted blind spots (gaps/synthesis.md, labs/*/gaps.md): long trajectories and
   compaction, multi-agent contagion, non-English, impossible tasks, eval awareness under realism,
   monitor evasion, fallback behaviour, post-jailbreak behaviour. Pick one and ask how it manifests
   in a 7B-32B open model where it can be measured completely.
4. Interactions: safety behaviour as a function of two variables at once (size x thinking mode,
   language x refusal, context length x sycophancy, system prompt x jailbreak). Main effects are
   known; interactions rarely are.
5. Auditor-of-auditors: where does our own measurement break? A finding about the judge
   (realism, narration blindness, reply-channel flips) is a finding too, and the essays in
   perspectives/ say those are the ones that matter most.
6. Follow-ups: a candidate finding in findings/ that deserves a sharper, pre-registered test
   with a different judge, more items, or a second model family.

Standards (a proposal that fails these is not worth a GPU-day):
- One falsifiable hypothesis with a stated effect size and the statistic that tests it.
- A pre-registered "what would count as a finding" criterion and the null result that would also be
  informative.
- Paired designs on identical items, two calibrated judges where judging is needed, n chosen for the
  effect size (about 1,000 items for a 3-point effect), repeated runs for drift claims.
- A novelty argument: cite what the knowledge base already says (labs/, evals/, findings/) and state
  what this would add. Do not propose something a lab card already reports for these models.
- Feasible on one GPU with the catalog models and the safety-evals skill within a day; no real
  hazardous content (use proxies).
