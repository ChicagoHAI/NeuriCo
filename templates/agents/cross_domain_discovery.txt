STEP 2A: Supplement the Topic Search with Transferable Methods
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Use this supplement when the research question has a methodological gap that
methods from another field could address. Respect explicit source/domain limits
and the current invocation's objective. If outside-domain discovery is out of
scope, or no search budget remains, record that and continue with the topic results.

1. Preserve the topic results before expanding.
   Record the accepted topic papers and existing resources in resources.md, using
   DOI, a stable URL, or title/year to identify each paper. Keep these entries and
   their evidence when adding methods. A shorter reading shortlist must not remove
   them from the resource catalog. Merge duplicate records while retaining their
   source trails; record corrections explicitly rather than silently dropping work.

2. Rewrite queries around the problem, while retaining the original queries.
   Describe the task's target, available observations, sample/data constraints,
   desired method properties, and assumptions. Formulate a complementary query
   without requiring the application-domain name. For example, early battery-life
   prediction with few cells and correlated features suggests "small-sample
   regression with correlated predictors and interpretable variable selection".
   Preserve substantive constraints; do not merely delete the domain word or
   assume a familiar algorithm is the answer. Use terminology from retrieved
   methods to refine a query only if it addresses an unresolved gap.

3. Trace citations with evidence of their purpose.
   From a relevant seed paper, inspect the passage surrounding a promising
   reference. Distinguish method use/adaptation, conceptual contribution,
   comparison, data/tool use, background, and unclear attribution. Follow a
   reference when that passage suggests a concrete contribution to the gap;
   citation count or outside-domain origin alone is insufficient. Record the
   citing paper, cited paper, passage location and attribution uncertainty.
   Check the cited source's own abstract or method passage when accessible.
   Keep the independent rewritten-query route: citations need not expose methods
   that have not yet been used in the target field.

4. Judge and append additions using source evidence.
   For each candidate, identify the method capability, its concrete use in this
   task, corresponding inputs/outputs, and assumptions or mismatches. Judge
   transferable-method fit separately from topic similarity. Include useful
   methods without forcing an outside-domain quota or a top-three position.
   In literature_review.md and resources.md, record the discovery query or citation
   path, stable source link, short supporting passage and location, evidence level
   (full-text passage, abstract, or search-only), proposed use and limitation.
   Mark insufficient evidence as "needs verification"; a failed source fetch is
   not proof of irrelevance. Distinguish inferred applicability from demonstrated
   target-domain results. Report new source papers separately from new methods
   already represented in the topic literature.

Keep the supplement bounded by the remaining task budget. Unless the task grants
a different expansion budget, use at most two additional search queries total
(including reference lookups), inspect citation contexts from at most two seed
papers, and open at most four additional candidate-source pages. Reuse acquired
text and deduplicate before further reading. These are caps within the overall
budget, not permission to extend it. Record actual expansion work and remaining
uncertainty, then continue with the combined catalog. This procedure uses the
available search tools and source reading; it does not require a new embedding
service or a recursive citation crawl.
