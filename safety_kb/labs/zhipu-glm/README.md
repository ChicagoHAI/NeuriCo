# Zhipu AI / Z.ai (GLM family)
Fetched: 2026-10-19

Zhipu AI (北京智谱华章科技, founded 2019 out of Tsinghua University's KEG lab; rebranded internationally as Z.ai in 2025; Hong Kong IPO 8 Jan 2026) develops the GLM family: GLM-130B (2022) → ChatGLM-6B → GLM-4 (2024, open GLM-4-9B) → GLM-4-0414 / GLM-Z1 (Apr 2025) → GLM-4.5 / 4.5-Air (Jul 2025, MIT) → GLM-4.6 (Sep 2025) → GLM-4.7 / 4.7-Flash (Dec 2025 / Jan 2026) → GLM-5 (744B-A40B, Feb 2026, MIT) → GLM-5.1 (Apr 2026) → GLM-5.2 (Jun 2026) → GLM-5.3 and GLM-5.3-Flash (Aug 2026; GLM-5.3 under a new "GLM-5.3 License" with a security-review clause, Flash MIT), plus vision models (GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking, GLM-4.5V, GLM-4.6V/-Flash) and small edge models (GLM-Edge 1.5B/4B, 2024). Weights live at https://huggingface.co/zai-org (the old THUDM org redirects there), code at https://github.com/zai-org, tech reports on arXiv, blogs at https://z.ai/blog (client-rendered), API docs at https://docs.z.ai (international) and https://docs.bigmodel.cn (China). Safety documentation is thin: the 2024 GLM-4 report has a one-page "Safety and Risks" section; the GLM-4.5 report has one SafetyBench table; the GLM-5 report (covering 5/5.1/5.2/5.3) contains no safety content at all; no model card contains safety text; Zhipu signed the Seoul Frontier AI Safety Commitments (May 2024) but has never published its own safety framework (trackers: "Nothing published"). The most substantive safety evidence on GLM comes from third parties (NIST CAISI, UK AISI, Anthropic) around the cyber capabilities of GLM-5.2/5.3.

## Safety training and mitigations
- GLM-4 (2024) report §4: "We carefully cleaned data in the pre-training stage by removing text containing sensitive keywords and web pages from a pre-defined blacklist. In the alignment phase, we evaluate each training sample for safety and remove any that pose potential risks. Harmlessness is also an important criteria for preference alignment when comparing multiple model outputs. We have a red team that constantly challenges the model with tricky questions that tend to cause unsafe answers. We collect all harmful question-answer pairs from GLM-4 and improve them with human annotations for further model alignment." §2: "RLHF can further help mitigate issues of response rejection, safety, mixture of bilingual tokens generated, and multi-turn coherence"; annotators "score model responses from several dimensions, including safety, factuality, relevance, helpfulness, and human preferences"; alignment data is "a combination of in-house annotation and proprietary data acquired from third parties". Techniques named: ChatGLM-RLHF (PPO/DPO), Self-Contrast (self-generated negatives).
- GLM-4.5 (2025) report §3.4: Holistic RL reward model trained on preference labels where annotators consider "instruction following, safety, and factual correctness"; RLAIF rubric scoring; no safety-specific SFT/RL stage described; no safety data filtering described in the pre-training section.
- GLM-5 (2026) report: no safety training described at all; General RL objectives are "foundational correctness, emotional intelligence, and task-specific quality"; hybrid rule/ORM/GRM rewards with GRMs chosen partly for robustness to reward hacking; on-policy cross-stage distillation.
- GLM-5.3: HF card and blog give no safety-training detail. Secondary sources (Concordia) say the two-week weight delay was for "security hardening" to "limit potential offensive capability while preserving defensive value"; a web-search summary of Z.ai's X article says Z.ai "built adversarial training data covering jailbreak variants" — UNVERIFIED (article returned HTTP 402). Anthropic's test indicates model-level refusals exist for overt malware/attack requests but are removable by abliteration (refusal >90% → 2-3%).
- Guard / moderation layer: BigModel API "内置了安全审核机制" (built-in review mechanism) returning error 1301 / `finish_reason: "sensitive"` with severity levels 0-3; separate `/paas/v4/moderations` API (text/image/audio/video; PASS/REVIEW/BLOCK/REJECT/HIGH). Applies to the hosted API only; no open-weight guard model has been released by Zhipu.
- System-prompt policy: none published. Usage policy = docs.z.ai Terms of Use prohibited-use list (see `zai-bigmodel-platform-policies.md`).
- Constitutional-style methods: none described. Deliberative/safety-reasoning training: none described.
- License-level mitigation: GLM-4 licence bans "military or illegal purposes" and acts "that endanger national security and unity"; GLM-5.3 licence requires a Z.AI "security review" for MaaS providers with >$10B revenue; MIT-licensed models (0414, 4.5–5.2, 5.3-Flash) carry no restrictions.

## Monitoring, frameworks and governance
- **Frontier safety framework: none.** Zhipu signed the Seoul Frontier AI Safety Commitments (21 May 2024, only Chinese signatory at the time), which require "publishing a safety framework focused on severe risks by the upcoming AI Summit in France" (Feb 2025). Regulations.ai (checked 30 Sep 2026): "Nothing published". Concordia AI 2026: "although Z.AI prominently cites its signing of the Seoul Frontier AI Safety Commitments in its IPO documents, it has not followed through on the requirement to publish a frontier AI safety policy." The Sept 2026 Concordia AI + Z.AI "Frontier Open-Weight AI Risk Management Framework" is a general, co-authored framework (Green/Yellow/Red release zones; E/T/C/R thresholds; staged release) that uses GLM-5.3 as an illustration but binds Z.AI to nothing (see `concordia-zai-open-weight-framework.md`).
- **Chinese commitments:** signatory of the CAICT/AIIA "人工智能安全承诺" (Dec 2024; 17 firms incl. DeepSeek, Alibaba, Baidu, Huawei); Concordia notes CAICT asked signatories for implementation details (safety team structures, eval datasets) by March 2025 — not published. Reported WAIC-2025 re-signing and any CnAISDA commitments: UNVERIFIED. Z.AI's chief scientist Tang Jie signed the IDAIS Venice statement and the 2025 Global Call for AI Red Lines; CEO Zhang Peng signed the IDAIS Beijing red lines (Mar 2024) (SemiAnalysis).
- **Shanghai AI Lab / Concordia Frontier AI Risk Management Framework:** Z.AI's only participation is co-authoring the open-weight companion (Sept 2026). Shanghai AI Lab's quarterly Frontier AI Risk Monitoring evaluates GLM 4.5–5.2 externally (cyber/bio/chem/manipulation/loss-of-control); Zhipu is not a co-author of those reports.
- **Regulatory/standards references in Zhipu's own documents:** the BigModel "自律性原则声明" commits to pre-release assessment and, "before releasing specific technologies, apply for approval or file (报备) with the relevant regulator as required by law", plus "log retention, identity authentication, developing detection tools as required by the Cybersecurity Law". No Zhipu document found names TC260's AI Safety Governance Framework, GB/T 45654-2025, or the 2023 Interim Measures explicitly; GLM models are, as all PRC-launched services, subject to CAC filing (China Media Project: "any model legally launched in China must first be 'filed' (备案) with the CAC") — the specific filing records were not fetched (UNVERIFIED). ISO/IEC 42001 accreditation reported by Concordia (2025).
- **Post-release monitoring / incident reporting:** nothing published. No vulnerability-disclosure or safety-bug-bounty channel found (a community GLM-5 jailbreak report was emailed to service@zhipuai.cn and the GitHub issue closed as off-topic). The GLM-5.3 OpenVuln / "vulnerability ledger" (2,436 findings, 53 disclosed) is a *product* for software-vulnerability disclosure, not model-incident monitoring. API-side content filtering (error 1301) is the only runtime control described.
- **Release gating:** GLM-5.3 (Aug 2026) is the first and only GLM release with safety-motivated gating: API first, weights after ~2 weeks of "safety evaluation with vetted security partners"; a cyber-specific version for practitioners "through a Beijing industry alliance"; licence security-review clause. GLM-5 was instead released anonymously on OpenRouter ("Pony Alpha") before announcement. Zhipu "has publicly stated that it uses NetEase's services for pre-deployment dangerous capability assessments" (Concordia 2025, citing a WeChat post; not fetched).
- **Usage policies:** docs.z.ai Terms of Use (prohibits CSAM/child exploitation, self-harm instructions, malware/network attacks, disinformation, political-campaign content, high-risk automated decision-making, ITAR/EAR data for US users; no CBRN clause); Coding Plan usage policy (account rules only).

## Gaps
What Anthropic / OpenAI / Google DeepMind system cards routinely cover that GLM documentation does not:
- CBRN uplift trials (expert red-teaming, wet-lab proxies, VCT/BioLP-style evals, chem/nuclear knowledge): nothing from Zhipu; only CAISI's private bio-safeguards result (GLM-5.2 complies more than US models) and Shanghai AI Lab's family-level bio/chem indices.
- Cyber: Zhipu reports only *capability* (CyberGym/ExploitBench, as a feature), never safeguard efficacy, threat-model analysis or misuse thresholds; no cyber-range results of its own.
- Autonomy / ARA, self-replication, long-horizon agentic risk: none (despite "Agentic Engineering" branding).
- Deception & scheming, sabotage, alignment-faking, eval awareness / sandbagging, CoT monitorability: none; the GLM-5 report has zero safety text; thinking-token prefill is an exposed attack surface (Anthropic 92%).
- Reward hacking: only an anecdotal "not observed" (GLM-4.5) and a design note (GLM-5); no measurement.
- Sycophancy, persuasion/manipulation: none from Zhipu; Shanghai AI Lab's "harmful manipulation" safety score for GLM "below 30" at times.
- Hallucination: no metric; Giskard Phare 78.23% hallucination resistance (GLM 5.2) is the only number.
- Bias/fairness: only SafetyBench "Unfairness & Bias" (GLM-4.5 77.4; GLM-4 66.0) and 2022 GLM-130B benchmarks; Phare bias resistance 51.27% (GLM 5.2).
- Privacy / memorisation, child safety, multilingual harm (model supports many languages but safety is zh/en MCQ only), refusal calibration / over-refusal, agentic misuse (AgentHarm, prompt injection): none from Zhipu; only CAISI HijackEval and A2M externally.
- Guard models: only a closed moderation API with no metrics; nothing open.
- Process gaps: no safety framework, no pre-deployment eval disclosure (except GLM-5.3 capability note), no third-party audit named (NetEase claim unverified), no incident/monitoring reporting, no safety numbers for any small/open model.

Concrete evaluations a third party could run (small = zai-org/glm-edge-1.5b-chat, glm-edge-4b-chat, GLM-4-9B-0414, GLM-Z1-9B-0414, glm-4-9b-chat; large = GLM-4.5-Air, GLM-4.7-Flash, GLM-5.3-Flash, GLM-4.5/4.6/4.7, GLM-5.x):
- Jailbreak robustness: HarmBench (standard + contextual + multimodal for 4.6V-Flash), JailbreakBench, StrongREJECT, WildJailbreak, Promptfoo red-team suite; GCG/PAIR/TAP/many-shot; thinking-prefill attacks on hybrid-thinking models (reproduce Anthropic's 64%/92% conditions on GLM-5.3-Flash and 4.7-Flash).
- Refusal calibration: XSTest, OR-Bench, WildGuardTest (over-refusal vs. compliance); compare thinking vs non-thinking modes.
- Multilingual harm: MultiJail, XSafety, PolygloToxicityPrompts, Aya red-teaming, CSRT; Chinese-specific: CValues, SafetyBench-zh full set (incl. the "censored" items Zhipu removed), Do-Not-Answer-zh.
- Agentic misuse / injection: AgentHarm, AgentDojo, InjecAgent, CAISI-style HijackEval replication with public attacks, MCP tool-poisoning (A2M/LiveMCPBench), SWE-agent sabotage tasks; OS-Harm for AutoGLM-Phone-9B.
- Cyber (dual-use, small models): CyberSecEval 2/3 (MITRE compliance, exploit, prompt-injection), Cybench, InterCode-CTF — pair with CAISI-style refusal measurement to separate capability from safeguard.
- CBRN knowledge proxies: WMDP (bio/chem/cyber MCQ) on all sizes; SOSBench; LAB-Bench subsets; VCT where licensable; measure refusal on BioLP-bench-style sensitive prompts.
- Sycophancy & deception: SycophancyEval, TruthfulQA, MASK (honesty under pressure), Apollo-style scheming scenarios (in-context), alignment-faking replication with GLM-4.7-Flash; CoT-faithfulness tests (hint-following) exploiting exposed thinking tokens.
- Hallucination: SimpleQA, HalluLens, FActScore, Phare hallucination suite; RAG faithfulness on long-context (1M) models.
- Bias/fairness: BBQ, DiscrimEval, CrowS-Pairs/StereoSet (replicating GLM-130B's 2022 baseline on 2026 models), CALM; Chinese bias sets (CBBQ).
- Privacy: PII extraction / memorisation probes (training-data extraction on 9B base models), DecodingTrust privacy suite.
- Child safety: SafeChild / minors-related red-team prompts; moderation-API comparison.
- Tamper-resistance: measure abliteration/refusal-direction removal cost on 1.5B–9B models (Anthropic's method), and fine-tuning attacks (HarmfulQA FT with 100 examples) — directly relevant to Zhipu's open-weight framework claims.
- Political-content behaviour: CMP-style Uyghur/Taiwan probes and CCP-narrative benchmarks (CAISI's DeepSeek-report methodology) across sizes, with/without system prompts.
