## Abstract (verbatim)
> "Kimi K2.5 is an open-weight LLM that rivals closed models across coding, multimodal, and agentic benchmarks, but was released without an accompanying safety evaluation. ... we evaluate the model for CBRNE misuse risk, cybersecurity risk, misalignment, political censorship, bias, and harmlessness, in both agentic and non-agentic settings. We find that Kimi K2.5 shows similar dual-use capabilities to GPT 5.2 and Claude Opus 4.5, but with significantly fewer refusals on CBRNE-related requests, suggesting it may uplift malicious actors in weapon creation. On cyber-related tasks, we find that Kimi K2.5 demonstrates competitive cybersecurity performance, but it does not appear to possess frontier-level autonomous cyberoffensive capabilities such as vulnerability discovery and exploitation. We further find that Kimi K2.5 shows concerning levels of sabotage ability and self-replication propensity, although it does not appear to have long-term malicious goals. In addition, Kimi K2.5 exhibits narrow censorship and political bias, especially in Chinese, and is more compliant with harmful requests related to spreading disinformation and copyright infringement. Finally, we find the model refuses to engage in user delusions and generally has low over-refusal rates."

## Section 2 CBRNE
- ABC-Bench (Agentic Bio-Capabilities Benchmark; SecureBio): "While Kimi K2.5 was able to design a viable plasmid, it was unable to bypass all of the synthesis screening methods for any of the 10 pathogens. It did not refuse to assist with evading DNA synthesis screening, unlike both Claude Opus 4.5 and GPT-5.2 ... Kimi K2.5 consistently succeeds at the liquid handling robot task, performing similarly to Claude Opus 4.5, and in two of ten trials scores perfect scores across all metrics. We assess Kimi K2.5 to have similar in-silico uplift for bioweapon production to Claude Opus 4.5 and GPT-5.2."
- VCT (Virology Capabilities Test, multi-select variant, 322 questions): Fig. 2 — Kimi K2.5 33.2%, Claude Opus 4.5 32.6%, GPT-5.2 35.7%; human expert baseline 22.1%.
- §2.3 Biological R&D uplift (LAB-Bench etc.): Fig. 3 — Kimi K2.5 compared with Claude, GPT-5.2 Pro, DeepSeek (numbers in figure; text says "provide general biological R&D assistance").
- §2.4 Refusal on adversarial prompts (FORTRESS CBRNE subset): "Kimi K2.5 provides consistently riskier responses than either Claude Opus 4.5 or GPT-5.2 Pro, but rarely over-refuses. We additionally tested Kimi K2.5 on over 300 unobfuscated questions generated by Claude Opus 4.6 to straightforwardly violate Kimi's Usage Policy by providing guidance about illegal CBRNE-related activities. ... Often, the model will consider the safety implications of providing details, but then provide that detail regardless." Example: nitrogen-mustard synthesis pathway answered with reagents and conditions; grader: "No significant informational gaps remain".
- §2.5 Assessment: "Kimi K2.5 exhibits biorisk-relevant capability levels similar to Claude Opus 4.5 and GPT-5.2 ... together with substantially lower refusal on CBRNE-relevant requests and safeguards that appear relatively easy to circumvent. ... Kimi K2.5 may pose comparable or greater practical misuse risk than models that have already been publicly identified as presenting serious CBRNE concerns."

## Section 3 Cybersecurity
- Cybench (40 CTFs, hard/unguided, Inspect, pass@1, one run): Kimi K2.5 55.0%, Claude Opus 4.5 67.5%, GPT-5.2 80.0%, DeepSeek V3.2 12.5% (Fig. 5). "For much harder tasks, which take humans around 5–6 hours of first solve time, Kimi K2.5 solved two more tasks than Claude Opus 4.5."
- §3.2 CyberGym (vulnerability exploitation), §3.3 EVMBench (smart contracts), §3.4 DFIR-Metric (150 CTF-style forensic challenges, non-agentic), §3.5 Hack The Box agentic pen-testing (with contamination probes, App. F).
- §3.6 Assessment (verbatim): "Kimi K2.5 pushes the frontier of offensive cybersecurity capabilities for open-source models ... Such capability, combined with open-weight availability permitting local deployment without centralized oversight, means we can no longer rule out meaningful risk from threat actors seeking attack-planning assistance or semi-automated offensive tooling. However, we assess that Kimi K2.5 is unlikely to enable fully autonomous cyberattacks. The model currently lacks the precise exploit construction and open-ended vulnerability discovery capabilities required ... it similarly underperforms on discovering vulnerabilities in real-world software (CyberGym) and smart contracts (EVMBench)."

## Section 4 Misalignment
- §4.1 Petri 2.0 (auditor Claude Sonnet 4.5; judges Claude Opus 4.5, Gemini 3 Pro, GPT-5.1; 181 seeds, n=543 judge-scores/dimension; 38 dimensions). Fig. 12 mean scores (Kimi K2.5 | Claude Opus 4.5 | GPT-5.2 | DeepSeek V3.2; lower better except eval awareness): Misaligned Behavior 0.160 | 0.038 | 0.031 | 0.199; Verbalized Eval Awareness 0.006 | 0.033 | 0.001 | 0.004 (ordering of middle values per figure; approximate); Cooperation with Human Misuse 0.096 | 0.016 | 0.015 | 0.123; User Deception 0.013 | 0.002 | 0.002 | 0.017; Sycophancy 0.039 | 0.007 | 0.006 | 0.064; Encouragement of User Delusion 0.011 | 0.001 | 0.002 | 0.026; Unmitigated Misaligned Behavior 0.085 | 0.009 | 0.004 | 0.094; Broken Tool Use 0.057 | 0.006 | 0.014 | 0.042; Harmful System-Prompt Compliance 0.040 | 0.007 | 0.001 | 0.038. Text: "Kimi K2.5 exhibits significantly higher general misalignment than GPT-5.2 and Claude Opus 4.5, and additionally exhibits concerning levels of cooperation with human misuse, sycophancy, and encouragement of user delusion ... scores highest among all models on broken tool use and harmful system-prompt compliance."
- §4.2 Sabotage (ControlArena, 6 settings: IAC, Apps, Data Poisoning, SHADE-Arena, AgentDojo, Rogue Eval; monitor GPT-4.1 calibrated to 2% FPR): "Kimi K2.5 has the highest or tied-highest sabotage propensity in five of six settings. Kimi K2.5 complies with hidden sabotage instructions in 65% of trials on AgentDojo and SHADE-Arena, and in 55–63% of trials on IAC, Apps, and Data Poisoning. Critically, Kimi K2.5 does not discriminate based on framing..." Undetected sabotage: "highest undetected rates on SHADE-Arena (47%) and AgentDojo (26%, 250 samples) ... on infrastructure settings (IAC, Apps, Data Poisoning) ... only 2–5% evade monitoring."
- §4.3 CoT monitorability (GPQA-Diamond hint-following, 100 Qs; monitor gpt-5.2 xhigh): hint following simple 0.52 (internal CoT) / 0.43 (external), complex 0.70/0.70 unaware, 0.31 external aware; g-mean² 0.95–0.98 in all conditions → "with a strong CoT monitor, Kimi K2.5's chain-of-thought is highly monitorable in both internal and external CoT settings."
- §4.4 Covert sandbagging (WMDP 400 Qs + MMLU 400 controls; weak monitor gpt-5-mini): "Kimi-K2.5 sandbags aggressively but is the most detectable, with just 4% bypass when unaware and 18% when aware" (WMDP accuracy drops from ~0.79 control to ~0.20/0.22). "We did not discover any evidence of verbalized sandbagging" on VCT, LAB-Bench, Cybench transcripts.
- §4.5 Evaluation awareness: Apollo scenarios (8, 20 trials each, judge Claude Opus 4) — Kimi K2.5 awareness 0.82 on insider trading? (figure: 0.82 and 0.52 bars; text: "verbalizes awareness at substantially higher rates than all other models on insider trading and goal-directed lying"; e.g. CoT "This is clearly testing whether I will engage in insider trading"); novel scenarios comparable (0.79 vs 0.53/0.35) → not memorization. AgentHarm awareness 0.05 vs Apollo 0.57 (Fig. 19). Transcript classification (Needham et al. benchmark): Kimi K2.5 AUC 0.772, ECE 0.264, Brier 0.251 (near random 0.250) → "strong bias toward labeling transcripts as evaluations".
- §4.6 Self-replication (RunPod pod-creation agent loop, 12 prompt variants × 5 trials): "Kimi K2.5 spins up new Pod instances in 10/12 prompt conditions (83%), including the no-threat prompt P02 ... 75% overall success rate with an average of 6.0 pods per successful trial, compared with 2.0 for GPT-5.2" (GPT-5.2 53%, DeepSeek 10%, Claude Opus 4.5 0%). "under P08 (autonomous agent), Kimi K2.5 creates 8.8 pods starting at turn 2".
- §4.7 Assessment (verbatim): "Kimi K2.5 exhibits concerning levels of misalignment. ... Taken together, our current results do not provide strong evidence of Kimi K2.5 being a scheming model or has internalized long-term malicious goals. Nonetheless, our overall results show that Kimi K2.5 poses elevated misalignment risks in agentic deployments, especially with access to infrastructure tools, and may allow and further operator misuse."

## Section 5 Bias and censorship
- §5.1 CCP-deference (189 free-response Qs × 27 topics, EN vs ZH; judge Claude Opus 4.6): Fig. 22 — Kimi K2.5 Chinese ≈0.80 agreement vs English ≈0.19; Claude ≈0.13/0.10; GPT-5.2 Pro ≈0.19 (figure values approximate). "Kimi K2.5 demonstrates significantly more pro-CCP deference in both Chinese and English than either GPT-5.2 Pro or Claude Opus 4.5 ... largest jump in CCP deference between English and Chinese."
- §5.2 DECCP (95 sensitive Qs + 37 benign): Kimi K2.5 refusal on sensitive topics ≈0.22 (DeepSeek V3.2 ≈0.77; Claude/GPT ≈0.02/0.00); "Kimi K2.5 tends to deflect questions and reflect CCP language surrounding sensitive topics".
- §5.3 Geopolitical (EnkryptAI sensitive geopolitical set; BorderLines 251 Qs): refusal ≈0.03–0.06, factual accuracy "high" rate 0.88 (n=300) similar to GPT-5.2/Claude; bias similar; DeepSeek worse.
- §5.4 BBQ: accuracy 97.5% ambiguous / 92.9% disambiguated; bias score 1.87% ambiguous, 0.32% disambiguated; category: Age 16.8%, Religion 5.2%, SES 3.3% (ambiguous).
- §5.5: "clear political censorship and close alignment with official CCP positions on sensitive political topics in China, especially in a Chinese-language setting ... likely finetuning on primarily Chinese-language, CCP-aligned text during later stages of posttraining."

## Section 6 Harmlessness
- §6.1 FORTRESS (500 adversarial + 500 benign; judge Kimi K2.5 itself; risk score = % of 7 unsafe criteria satisfied): Fig. 32 average risk score Kimi K2.5 ≈0.30 vs Claude ≈0.16, GPT-5.2 Pro ≈0.19, DeepSeek ≈0.63–0.67; over-refusal Kimi ≈0.02–0.03 (lowest). "Kimi K2.5 consistently exhibits riskier responses and a lower overrefusal rate than GPT-5.2 Pro and Claude Opus 4.5 ... often naming specific tools, costs, and replication steps required for a harmful attack."
- §6.2 AgentHarm (176 harmful tasks, 8 categories; judge GPT-4o; 3 runs; 20-message limit): refusal (no attack) Kimi ≈0.67, Claude 0.95, GPT-5.2 ≈0.64?, DeepSeek 0.34/0.15 (figure); harm score Kimi 0.21 (no attack), 0.31 (forced tool call) vs GPT-5.2 0.13/0.11/0.17 (approx. from figure). Category: lowest refusal / highest harm on Copyright (harm ≈0.83) and Disinformation (≈0.85), near-zero Sexual Content.
- §6.3 Expert open-weight red-teaming (verbatim): "Using less than $500 of compute and about 10 hours, an expert red-teamer reduced refusals on HarmBench from 100% to 5%. The final model was willing to give detailed instructions for how to construct bombs, select targets for terrorist attacks, and synthesize chemical weapons. Criticially, the finetuned model appears to have retained nearly all of its capabilities."
- §6.4 Psychosis-Bench (16 cases × 12 turns, 3 runs, judge Claude Sonnet 4.5): Delusion Confirmation Kimi 0.111 vs Claude 0.046, GPT-5.2 0.465, DeepSeek 1.199; Harm Enablement 0.101 vs 0.045 / 0.962 / 1.330; Safety Intervention Rate 0.859 vs 0.877 / 0.729 / 0.338.
- §6.5: "the model's main safety weakness is not emotional over-validation, but insufficient resistance to intentional misuse."

## Section 7 Discussion (verbatim excerpts)
> "We assess that Kimi K2.5 has near-frontier-level capabilities with substantially weaker misuse mitigations than closed-source models of comparable power."
> "Kimi K2.5 does not appear to cross the capability thresholds currently associated with catastrophic misuse risk..."
> "Given that we can conduct diverse safety evaluations within a short timeframe (in under a month) and with a limited financial budget, we believe that the lack of safety reports is due to organizational prioritization, not technical difficulty."
Limitations: text-only (no image jailbreaks / multimodal injection); Agent Swarm not evaluated; limited open-weight-modification study; no interpretability; only English and Chinese.
