Fetched: 2026-10-19

## 1. CAICT / AI Industry Alliance "Artificial Intelligence Safety Commitments" (人工智能安全承诺), December 2024 -- DeepSeek SIGNED
- Carnegie Endowment, Scott Singer, Jan 2025: https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2025/01/deepseek-and-other-chinese-firms-converge-with-western-companies-on-ai-promises (local sources/deepseek/carnegie-deepseek-chinese-firms-ai-promises-2025-01.md). Verbatim: "Last month, DeepSeek joined sixteen other Chinese companies in signing onto the Artificial Intelligence Safety Commitments (人工智能安全承诺). While branded as a domestic Chinese initiative, the commitments bear strong similarity to ongoing global industry-led efforts ... piloted at last year's AI [Seoul Summit]". "The Chinese AI Safety Commitments offer a more comprehensive set of security requirements for data and critical infrastructures and have a specific line on adopting appropriate safety measures for open source initiatives, where Chinese products like DeepSeek's are especially competitive". "Only one Chinese company, Zhipu.ai, signed onto the Seoul Commitments".
- Concordia AI, State of AI Safety in China (2025), Section 5.1.1 (p.54, verbatim): "AIIA's arguably most significant AI safety action has been the launch of 'AI Safety Commitments' in December 2024, which 17 Chinese firms endorsed, including LLM startups DeepSeek, Zhipu AI, MiniMax, and 01.AI, and large technology companies such as Alibaba, Baidu, ByteDance, and Huawei. Signatories agreed to establish clear risk identification and mitigation processes throughout the AI development life cycle, pledging to: Allocate resources to dedicated safety teams; Build safety and security risk management mechanisms; Conduct rigorous safety and security testing, such as red teaming; Strengthen data security measures; Enhance security of AI software and hardware infrastructure; Boost transparency and enable external oversight; Advance frontier AI safety research, particularly for AI agents and embodied AI." "some leading LLM developers have not signed, including Moonshot AI and StepFun."
- Text of the commitments (English): ETO AGORA instrument 1831 https://agora.eto.tech/instrument/1831 (local sources/deepseek/eto-agora-caict-ai-safety-commitments-instrument-1831.md). Commitment I: "Establish safety teams or organizational structures and build safety and security risk management mechanisms. Designate leader responsible for AI safety and security ..."; Commitment II: "Conduct safety and security testing for AI models to enhance the performance, safety, and reliability. Through dedicated simulation and red-teaming experts to rigorously test AI models prior to their release or update. For large models in particular, prioritize safety and reliability evaluations ..."; further commitments on data security, infrastructure, transparency, research.
- No DeepSeek publication of any artefact fulfilling the transparency commitment (e.g., a safety report or framework) has been found other than the Nature R1 SI.

## 3. Concordia AI, "State of AI Safety in China (2025)" (99 pp, released at WAIC 29 Jul 2025)
- PDF: https://aisafetychina.substack.com/api/v1/file/0d3dfced-8d32-48ec-8d5c-6630a76c19cf.pdf (announcement https://aisafetychina.substack.com/p/state-of-ai-safety-in-china-report); local sources/deepseek/concordia-ai-state-of-ai-safety-in-china-2025.pdf (+.txt).
- Section 5.2.1 "Safety in technical model cards" (p.56-57, verbatim): "Out of the 13 companies surveyed, nine have published technical model cards on arXiv. Yet many of these contain minimal or no information on safety. Only three—01.AI's Yi-Lightning (Dec 2024), MiniMax's MiniMax-01 (Jan 2025), and Zhipu AI's GLM-4 (July 2024)—include a dedicated section on safety. Only three model cards include explicit safety evaluation results: DeepSeek V3, MiniMax-01, and GLM-4. Even in these cases, the discussion about what specific risks are being addressed remains rather vague. None of the papers disclose evaluations results for CBRN or loss of control risks."
- Table 5.1 rows (verbatim): "DeepSeek-V3 | December 2024 | arXiv Yes | Brief safety mentions: use constitutional AI; their reward-model design mitigates reward-hacking. | RewardBench, which has a 'safety' category." "DeepSeek-R1 | January 2025 | Yes | Brief safety mentions: RL reward models boost harmlessness; full-output risk checks. | None mentioned." (Written before the Sep 2025 Nature version added SI 4.3.)
- Section 5.3 research output (p.61, verbatim): "Newer LLM startups consistently had a smaller number of safety papers than large technology companies over the past year, with two papers from StepFun and Zhipu AI, one paper from 01.AI, and zero papers from DeepSeek, MiniMax, and Moonshot."
- p.52 (verbatim): "Alibaba Research Institute additionally references evaluations of DeepSeek's open source models by Western organizations for CBRN risks." CAICT published "DeepSeek Open-Source Revelation (Part 3): Typical Application Risks of Open-Source AI and Response Strategies" (footnote 232) -- not fetched.
- General finding: "Chinese AI developers typically implement well-known safety methods, but provide limited transparency on safety evaluations." China "continues to implement existing regulations, which require pre-deployment registration and safety assessments" (announcement text).

## 4. Anthropic, "Detecting and preventing distillation attacks" (23 Feb 2026)
- URL: https://www.anthropic.com/news/detecting-and-preventing-distillation-attacks ; local sources/deepseek/anthropic-detecting-and-preventing-distillation-attacks-2026-02.md
- Verbatim: "We have identified industrial-scale campaigns by three AI laboratories—DeepSeek, Moonshot, and MiniMax—to illicitly extract Claude's capabilities to improve their own models. These labs generated over 16 million exchanges with Claude through approximately 24,000 fraudulent accounts, in violation of our terms of service and regional access restrictions."
- DeepSeek section (verbatim): "Scale: Over 150,000 exchanges. DeepSeek generated synchronized traffic across accounts. Identical patterns, shared payment methods, and coordinated timing suggested 'load balancing' ... In one notable technique, their prompts asked Claude to imagine and articulate the internal reasoning behind a completed response and write it out step by step—effectively generating chain-of-thought training data at scale. We also observed tasks in which Claude was used to generate censorship-safe alternatives to politically sensitive queries".
- Safety framing (verbatim): "Illicitly distilled models lack necessary safeguards, creating significant national security risks ... Models built through illicit distillation are unlikely to retain those safeguards ... If distilled models are open-sourced, this risk multiplies".
- OpenAI: Jan 2025 statement that DeepSeek "may have inappropriately" distilled OpenAI outputs and a Feb 2026 memo to the House Select Committee are reported in press (search summaries) but the primary OpenAI documents were not fetched -- UNVERIFIED here. NIST CAISI explicitly scoped out distillation ("it does not investigate ... the possibility that they were trained on distilled data").
