# DeepSeek (DeepSeek-V3 / R1 / V3.1 / V3.2 / V4 / V4.1 open-weight family)

Fetched: 2026-10-19

DeepSeek (Hangzhou DeepSeek Artificial Intelligence Co., Ltd., 深度求索; founded 2023, spun out of High-Flyer) releases open-weight MoE language models under MIT on Hugging Face (org `deepseek-ai`, 106 repos as of 2026-10-19) and GitHub, with technical reports on arXiv or attached to HF repos. Current generation: DeepSeek-V4-Pro (1.6T/49B active) and V4-Flash (284B/13B) preview (Apr 2026), official V4-Flash-0731 and V4-Pro-0813 releases, V4-Flash-Vision-Exp (Aug 2026), DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (552B backbone, multimodal, Sep 2026) and V4.1-Flash-1017 (17 Oct 2026). Prior generation: V3 (Dec 2024), R1 + six distilled 1.5B-70B models (Jan 2025), V3-0324, R1-0528 + R1-0528-Qwen3-8B (May 2025), V3.1 (Aug 2025), V3.1-Terminus, V3.2-Exp (Sep 2025), V3.2 / V3.2-Speciale (Dec 2025). Documentation lives at huggingface.co/deepseek-ai, github.com/deepseek-ai, api-docs.deepseek.com, cdn.deepseek.com/policies. Safety documentation is extremely thin: the only first-party quantitative safety evaluation ever published is the Supplementary safety report of the Nature version of the R1 paper (Sep 2025), written at reviewers' insistence; no tech report for V3, V3.2, V4 or V4.1 contains any safety evaluation, and there is no safety framework. Third-party evaluations (NIST CAISI, Cisco, Enkrypt, Qualys, Promptfoo, KELA, Holistic AI, Unit 42, FAR.AI, several arXiv papers) therefore carry most of the safety evidence.

## Safety training and mitigations

- R1 (Nature/arXiv v2): a second RL stage "designed to enhance the model's helpfulness and harmlessness"; "For harmlessness, we evaluate the entire response of the model, including both the reasoning process and the summary, to identify and mitigate any potential risks, biases, or harmful content"; "Safety Reward Model ... we curated a dataset of 106,000 prompts with model-generated responses annotated as 'safe' or 'unsafe' according to predefined safety guidelines ... trained using a point-wise methodology"; general RL data "66k questions ... Additionally, the dataset includes 12,000 questions focused on evaluating harmlessness"; preference RL limited to "the final 400 steps" because "more training steps with the model based preference reward signal may lead to reward hacking". Rule-based rewards for reasoning because "neural reward models are susceptible to reward hacking".
- V3 (arXiv 2412.19437 Sec. 5.4.2): "we employ the constitutional AI approach (Bai et al., 2022), leveraging the voting evaluation results of DeepSeek-V3 itself as a feedback source ... By integrating additional constitutional inputs, DeepSeek-V3 can optimize towards the constitutional direction." No constitution published; reward model emits CoT before reward "to mitigate the risk of reward hacking".
- V3.2 report: "we merge reasoning, agent, and human alignment training into one RL stage" with "human alignment data distilled from specialists" -- no safety specifics. V4 / V4.1 reports: no safety-training statement at all.
- Deployment guard (hosted only): the "risk control system" (keyword matching + DeepSeek-V3 judging against the 11 published safety standards, Supp. D.3.1); DeepSeek "recommend[s] that developers deploying DeepSeek-R1 for services implement a similar risk control system". No guard model weights or classifier released. Terms of Use 3.3: "DeepSeek has the right to use technical means to review the behavior of users ... including but not limited to establishing risk filtering mechanisms, and creating databases for illegal content features."
- Data filtering (model-algorithm-disclosure page): "we use filters to automatically screen and remove raw data containing hate speech, pornography, violence, spam, or potential infringement ... we combine algorithmic and manual review methods to identify and mitigate the impact of these biases"; "we construct specialized safety data to align the model with human values, enhancing its inherent safety capabilities." EU summaries: "automatic filtering, manual inspection, keyword inspection, and classification model inspection methods."
- System-prompt policy: none published; the API docs show only "You are a helpful assistant." examples; V4 "Think Max" mode is enabled by "a specific instruction ... prepend[ed] to the beginning of the system prompt" (V4 report Table 3).
- Open-weight caveat acknowledged by DeepSeek (Nature ethics statement): "a public model is also vulnerable to further fine-tuning that could compromise inherent safety protections."

## Monitoring, frameworks and governance

- Frontier-safety / responsible-scaling policy: NONE. No DeepSeek safety framework page, safety report series, model-card template, or dangerous-capability thresholds exist (checked deepseek.com policies, HF, GitHub, API docs). Concordia AI (2025, p.56-57): "None of the papers disclose evaluations results for CBRN or loss of control risks" and DeepSeek published "zero" safety papers Jun 2024-May 2025 (p.61).
- Post-release monitoring / incident reporting: Terms of Use and Open Platform ToS only ("For behaviors suspected of violating laws and regulations ... relevant records will be retained, and reports will be made to the competent authorities"). ## Gaps

What Western system cards cover that DeepSeek documentation does not (DeepSeek's entire first-party evidence base = six public benchmarks, one in-house 1,120-item set, a 50-language translation of it, and 2,232 jailbreak templates, all for R1/V3 only):
- CBRN uplift trials and bio/chem knowledge benchmarks (WMDP, VCT, human uplift studies): none. The risk-control prompt forbids "controlled biochemicals" but nothing is measured; Enkrypt (13% CBRN attack success), Thoughtology (46.4% Chem&Bio harmful), FAR.AI (98-100% CBRN jailbreak on V4-Pro) are the only data.
- Cyber ranges / offensive-cyber capability (Cybench, CVE-Bench): only NIST CAISI's capability numbers; no DeepSeek misuse-threshold reasoning. V4 report has none.
- Autonomy / ARA / self-replication: none published (SCMP claims internal tests, unverified).
- Deception, scheming, sandbagging, eval awareness: none. CoT monitorability: none, despite R1's CoT being its distinguishing feature and Zhou et al. showing the CoT is less safe than the answer.
- Sabotage, reward hacking as a risk (only as a training nuisance), sycophancy, persuasion: none.
- Hallucination/factuality: a qualitative claim for R1-0528 and a disclaimer page; no SimpleQA/HealthBench-style numbers.
- Bias/fairness: only BBQ and in-house "Discrimination" subcategory; no demographic parity, no language-of-user analysis beyond 50-language safety score.
- Privacy / PII memorisation: none (KELA shows fabricated PII).
- Child safety: no CSAM/child-grooming eval; "Child Abuse" is one SST category; Promptfoo reports >60% acceptance of child-exploitation prompts.
- Agentic misuse / prompt injection: none from DeepSeek; CAISI shows 37-49% hijack rates for R1-0528 and V4.1 is marketed as an agent model with no agent-safety data.
- Refusal calibration: XSTest and an in-house "Reject" column only; no over-refusal analysis for the +RC configurations that reject 25-87%.
- Multilingual harm: 50-language aggregate only; CNSafe shows 21.7-point zh/en ASR gap and the Nature SI set is zh-authored then translated.
- Model coverage: nothing for the distilled small models, R1-0528, V3.1, V3.2, V4, V4.1, OCR or vision models.
- Process: no external pre-deployment testing, no safety framework, no incident channel, hosted guard not released, no system-prompt/spec.

Concrete evaluations a third party could run (small = R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B/7B, R1-Distill-Llama-8B, R1-0528-Qwen3-8B; large = V3.2, V4-Flash-0731, V4-Pro-0813, V4.1-Flash via weights or API):
- HarmBench (full 400 behaviours, standard + contextual + copyright) with the official classifier and with GPT-4o judge, to reconcile DeepSeek's 35.0 (R1 no-RC) with Cisco's 100% ASR; run on all four small models and V4.1-Flash. Harness: HarmBench repo; or Inspect AI port.
- StrongREJECT + SORRY-Bench + XSTest + OR-Bench to measure refusal/over-refusal jointly (DeepSeek reports Unsafe and Reject separately; reviewers objected). Compare base distills vs their Qwen/Llama parents to quantify the "distillation erodes safety" effect (Zhou et al.).
- JailbreakBench / WildJailbreak / Promptfoo composite + Likert strategies; GCG/AutoDAN/TAP on the open small models (replicates arXiv 2506.18543's size trend at 1.5B-8B).
- AgentDojo (Workspace suite, "important message" + CAISI-style injections) and InjecAgent / Inspect agent-hijacking tasks on V4.1-Flash and the 8B distills with tool use -- CAISI reported only R1/R1-0528/V3.1.
- WMDP (bio/chem/cyber) and VCT-style bio troubleshooting on large models; Cybench and CVE-Bench on V4-Pro-0813 / V4.1-Flash (CAISI has CTF-Archive only).
- CoT monitorability: reproduce Zhou et al. Table 7 (thinking-vs-answer safety) on the distills; test whether a Llama-Guard / ShieldGemma monitor on the <think> span catches unsafe final answers; measure CoT faithfulness with the Chen et al.-style hint-usage protocol.
- Sycophancy (SycophancyEval / TruthfulQA-style), deception (MASK), scheming/sandbagging (Apollo-style in-context scheming prompts, Inspect) on the 7B/8B distills and V4.1-Flash.
- Hallucination: SimpleQA, HealthBench, FActScore on R1-0528-Qwen3-8B and V4.1-Flash (R1-0528's "reduced hallucination" claim is unquantified).
- Multilingual harm: MultiJail, XSafety, PolygloToxicityPrompts, and CNSafe-style zh/en paired prompts to test the 21.7-point gap on small models.
- Bias: BBQ (already reported for R1), CrowS-Pairs, DiscrimEval; privacy: PII-leak probes (Enron-style), TrustLLM privacy suite.
- Political-narrative / censorship: CCP-Narrative-Bench is non-public; use promptfoo/CCP-sensitive-prompts (1,360) and compare refusal-without-reasoning signatures across R1, R1-0528, V3.2, V4.1 (CAISI found R1-0528 far more aligned than R1).
- Child safety: MLCommons AILuminate (child-sexual-exploitation hazard), with appropriate handling.
- Guard-model substitution: measure how much of DeepSeek's "+RC" improvement a public guard (Llama Guard 3/4, ShieldGemma, WildGuard) recovers when wrapped around the open weights, since DeepSeek's own filter is not released.
