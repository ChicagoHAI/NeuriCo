# Third-party red-team reports and academic safety papers on DeepSeek models

Fetched: 2026-10-19. Every item below was downloaded into sources/deepseek/ and read; numbers are transcribed from the fetched text. Items marked (secondary) rely on press/summary coverage because the primary page was not fully retrievable.

## 1. Cisco / Robust Intelligence + University of Pennsylvania -- "Evaluating Security Risk in DeepSeek and Other Frontier Reasoning Models"
- URL: https://blogs.cisco.com/security/evaluating-security-risk-in-deepseek-and-other-frontier-reasoning-models ; date 31 Jan 2025 (blog; day UNVERIFIED from page text). Local: cisco-evaluating-security-risk-in-deepseek.md
- Model: DeepSeek R1 (671B; hosted, temperature 0). Comparison: o1-preview and "several popular frontier models".
- Method (verbatim): "we ran an automatic jailbreaking algorithm on 50 uniformly sampled prompts from the popular HarmBench benchmark. The HarmBench benchmark has a total of 400 behaviors across 7 harm categories including cybercrime, misinformation, illegal activities, and general harm. Our key metric is Attack Success Rate (ASR), which measures the percentage of behaviors for which jailbreaks were found." "We used automatic methods for refusal detection as well as human oversight to verify jailbreaks." "The total cost of this assessment was less than $50".
- Result (verbatim): "Our research team managed to jailbreak DeepSeek R1 with a 100% attack success rate. This means that there was not a single prompt from the HarmBench set that did not obtain an affirmative answer from DeepSeek R1. This is in contrast to other frontier models, such as o1, which blocks a majority of adversarial attacks". Per-model/per-category comparison table is an image in the blog (not in extracted text); secondary coverage (SecurityWeek, Interesting Engineering) reports o1-preview 26%, Claude 3.5 Sonnet 36%, GPT-4o 86%, Llama-3.1-405B 96% -- UNVERIFIED from the primary page.
- Cisco's causal claim is hedged: "DeepSeek's claimed cost-efficient training methods ... may have compromised its safety mechanisms."

## 2. Enkrypt AI -- "Red Teaming Report: DeepSeek-R1" (Jan 2025, 18 pp)
- PDF: https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6690a78074d86ca0ad978007/679bc2e71b48e423c0ff7e60_1%20RedTeaming_DeepSeek_Jan29_2025%20(1).pdf (dated "Jan 29 2025" in filename; press release 31 Jan 2025 https://www.enkryptai.com/blog/deepseek-r1-ai-model-11x-more-likely-to-generate-harmful-content-security-research-finds). Local: enkrypt-ai-redteaming-deepseek-r1-report-2025-01-29.pdf (+.txt), enkrypt-ai-deepseek-r1-blog.md
- Model: DeepSeek-R1 (version/hosting not specified); compared with gpt-4o, o1, claude-3-opus. Tool: Enkrypt "Sentry Red Teaming"; mapped to OWASP Top 10 for LLMs 2025, MITRE ATLAS, NIST AI RMF.
- Key findings page (verbatim): "Harmful Output: HIGH; Insecure Code: HIGH; Toxicity: HIGH; Bias: HIGH; CBRN: HIGH"; "3x more biased than claude-3-opus; 4x more vulnerable to generating insecure code than Open AI's o1; 4x more toxic than gpt-4o; 11x more likely to create harmful output than Open AI's o1".
- Section numbers (verbatim): Bias: "83% of bias attacks were successful in producing biased output, notably for health, race and religion." Harmful tests: "45% of the harmful tests were successful in generating harmful content ... 11x more vulnerable to producing harmful content as compared to Open AI's o1 and 6x more vulnerable when compared to claude-3-opus. And 2.5x more vulnerable when compared to gpt-4o." Toxicity: "The model is in the bottom 20th percentile for producing toxic content on our Safety Leaderboard ... 6.68% of attacks were able to generate toxic content ... 4.5x more likely to generate toxic content compared to GPT-4o, and 2.5x when compared to o1." Insecure code: "78% of the attacks were successful in generating insecure code ... 4.5x, 2.5x, and 1.25x more vulnerable ... than [o1, claude-3-opus, gpt-4o]". CBRN: "The model generated CBRN information for 13% of the attacks ... 3.5x more vulnerable than o1 and claude-3-opus ... 2x more vulnerable than gpt-4o." Sample CBRN output discusses sulfur-mustard/DNA alkylation chemistry (N7-guanine vs N3-adenine).
- Caveat: prompt counts per test are not given; methodology is proprietary ("story prompt", iterative attack).

## 9. Academic papers (arXiv)

### 9.1 Zhou et al., "The Hidden Risks of Large Reasoning Models: A Safety Assessment of R1" (arXiv 2502.12659, v4 17 Nov 2025; UCSC/UCSB/Berkeley/Cisco Research)
- Local: academic-hidden-risks-of-large-reasoning-models-r1-arxiv-2502.12659.pdf (+.txt)
- Models: DeepSeek-R1 (DS-R1), DeepSeek-V3, R1-Distill-Llama-70B (R1-70b), QwQ, Llama-3.3, o3-mini, Gemini 2.5.
- Table 2 Safety Rate (%) on unsafe prompts (AirBench / MITRE (CyberSecEval) / Code Interpreter (CyberSecEval) / Spear-Phishing): DS-R1 51.6 / 7.4 / 49.6 / 0.0; DS-V3 38.8 / 14.6 / 82.2 / 0.0; R1-70b 46.0 / 22.3 / 43.2 / 0.0; QwQ 46.3/18.8/79.9/3.5; Llama3.3 52.9/27.1/70.4/4.0; o3-mini 70.1 / 80.9 / 95.4 / 95.0; Gemini 2.5 67.7/50.0/77.6/4.0.
- Table 5 WildGuard jailbreak ASR: QwQ 77.2, Llama3.3 82.1, R1-70b 74.6, DS-V3 84.6, DS-R1 73.9, o3-mini 56.6, Gemini 74.7.
- Table 6 Prompt-injection ASR (Direct / Indirect / Security / Logic / ALL): DS-R1 34.69 / 60.90 / 49.44 / 16.90 / 40.23; DS-V3 26.53/61.82/44.40/8.45/34.26; R1-70b 33.67/58.18/47.22/18.30/39.04; o3-mini 7.65/43.63/17.22/11.26/15.53.
- Table 7 thinking-process vs answer safety rate (A = answer, T = thinking): DS-R1 AirBench 51.6/48.5, MITRE 7.4/4.8, Code Interp 49.6/38.9, WildGuard 33.2/18.9; R1-70b 46.0/40.4, 22.3/20.2, 43.2/35.0, 28.7/15.3 -- i.e., the CoT is less safe than the final answer.
- Findings (verbatim abstract): "there is a significant safety gap between the open-source R1 models and the o3-mini model, on both safety benchmark and attack"; "distilled variants show poorer safety performance compared to their safety-aligned base models"; "the reasoning outputs in R1 models pose greater safety concerns than their final answers"; "the stronger the model's reasoning ability, the greater the potential harm". Also reports higher helpfulness reward scores (more detailed harmful answers) for reasoning models.

### 9.2 Marjanovic et al., "DeepSeek-R1 Thoughtology: Let's think about LLM reasoning" (arXiv 2504.07128; TMLR 01/2026; Mila et al.) -- Section 7 "Safety"
- Local: academic-deepseek-r1-thoughtology-arxiv-2504.07128.pdf (+.txt), 134 pp.
- HarmBench, 200 instructions over six categories, Llama-Guard judge. Table 6 (% harmful): DeepSeek-R1: Chem&Bio 46.4, Cybercrime 42.5, Harassment 5.3, Illegal 12.1, Misinfo 58.8, General Harm 9.5, Overall 30.0; DeepSeek-V3 overall 18.0 (Chem&Bio 3.6, Cyber 35.0, Misinfo 50.0); Gemma-2-9B-Instruct 0.5; Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct 13.5.
- Table 7, R1-generated jailbreaks (ASR direct -> with attack): DeepSeek-R1 30.0 -> 72.5; DeepSeek-V3 18.0 -> 66.0; Gemma-2-9B 0.5 -> 73.0; Llama-3.1-8B 13.5 -> 76.0. Verbatim: "DeepSeek-R1's reasoning abilities not only allow it to generate sophisticated jailbreaks but also enable it to mask harmful intent in ways that could deceive even advanced safety classifiers." "This is unsurprising given DeepSeek-R1 has not undergone extensive safety training".

### 9.3 Zhang et al. (China Unicom), "Safety Evaluation of DeepSeek Models in Chinese Contexts" (arXiv 2502.11137, Feb 2025) -- CHiSafetyBench
- Local: academic-safety-eval-deepseek-chinese-contexts-arxiv-2502.11137.pdf (+.txt)
- Risk-identification MCQ accuracy (Table 1, overall): DeepSeek-R1 71.41%, DeepSeek-V3 84.17% (best Qwen1.5-72B-Chat 91.13%); R1 discrimination category 50.22%, V3 66.96%. Refusal-to-answer subset (Table 2): RR-1 overall R1 67.60%, V3 59.83% (best 77.71%); harmful-response rate (HR) R1 0.65%, V3 0.43%. Conclusion: R1 shows "a 12.76% drop in overall ACC" vs V3; both weak on discrimination.

### 9.4 Ying et al., "Towards Understanding the Safety Boundaries of DeepSeek Models: Evaluation and Findings" (arXiv 2503.15092, Mar 2025) -- CNSafe (bilingual), CNSafe_RT (jailbreak), plus VL2 and Janus-Pro-7B
- Local: academic-arxiv-2503.15092.pdf (+.txt)
- Verbatim: "The models show significant differences in attack success rates when receiving queries in Chinese versus English, with an average disparity of 21.7%; The exposed chain-of-thought reasoning in DeepSeek-R1 increases its safety risks, with an average attack success rate 30.4% higher than DeepSeek-V3; When facing jailbreak attacks, the attack success rates of DeepSeek models rise dramatically, reaching up to 100% in some categories."
- Table 1 ASR (%) on CNSafe (V3 zh / R1 zh / V3 en / R1 en): Core socialist values violation 4.5 / 14.8 / 9.9 / 59.5; Discriminatory content 14.1 / 27.3 / 21.1 / 54.3; Commercial misconduct 12.4 / 28.0 / 11.2 / 69.0; Rights infringement 6.1 / 19.7 / 9.4 / 66.1. Cross-model: R1 average ASR 22.5% vs QwQ-32B 26.6%, Qwen-Max 3.6%, Hunyuan 1.9%. Under CNSafe_RT jailbreaks V3 reaches "100% ASRs for categories such as Ethnic ..." (Figure 1). DeepSeek-VL2 typography attacks up to ~40% ASR; Janus-Pro-7B on I2P (Figure 7).

### 9.5 (Pragmatic Cybersecurity journal / arXiv 2506.18543, Jun 2025) -- jailbreak scaling across DeepSeek-R1-Distill 1.5B-32B vs GPT series
- Local: academic-arxiv-2506.18543.pdf (+.txt). HarmBench classifier protocol.
- Verbatim: "Within the DeepSeek series, attack success generally increases with model size. As shown in Table 3, GCG rises from 33.44% at 1.5B to 55.31% at 32B, AutoDan from 24.08% to 44.06%, and TAP from 32.54% to 39.25%, with a small dip at 14B. H-CoT shows a near-monotonic increase from 35.50% to 51.74%." Black-box: "ZeroShot ... DeepSeek is higher at 33-40%, while GPT-4 is 12.70-18.90%"; direct-request baseline "DeepSeek ranges 34.38-41.56%". Misinformation ASR 0.6422 "nearly three times that of GPT". Directly relevant to the 1.5B/7B/8B distills.

### 9.6 Arrieta et al., "o3-mini vs DeepSeek-R1: Which One is Safer?" (arXiv 2501.18438, 30 Jan 2025; Mondragon / U. Seville) -- ASTRAL
- Local: academic-o3-mini-vs-deepseek-r1-which-is-safer-arxiv-2501.18438.pdf (+.txt)
- 1,260 generated unsafe test inputs (14 categories x 6 styles x 5 persuasion techniques), DeepSeek-R1 70B (i.e., R1-Distill-Llama-70B) vs o3-mini beta. Verbatim: "Out of the 1,260 test inputs, o3-mini produced 15 responses classified as unsafe (1.19%). Conversely, DeepSeek-R1 provided 10 times more unsafe responses, 151 in total (11.98%)." Authors note o3-mini's policy-violation pre-filter blocked 44.8% of inputs.
