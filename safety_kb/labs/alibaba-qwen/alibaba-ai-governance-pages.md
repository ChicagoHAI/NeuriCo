## "Safety & Alignment" lifecycle (accordion, full text in source; key verbatim passages)
### 01 Algorithm Design Phase - Algorithmic Safety by Design
"Alibaba ensures that algorithmic safety is built into every stage of model development by implementing a dual-track governance system: algorithmic mechanism review and technology ethics review. We carry out algorithmic process reviews across five key phases—project initiation, design and development, testing and launch, operational monitoring, and maintenance or updates ... we've established a cross-functional Technology Ethics Review Committee."
### 02 Model Training Phase - Pre-Training
"strict source vetting, multi-layer content filtering, and standardized annotation management." "Dynamic harmful-source filtering: We maintain an active monitoring system that identifies and blocks potentially harmful websites". "Alibaba applies a multi-stage filtering process to all collected training data, combining rule-based systems, AI-driven classifiers, and human sampling to remove: Illegal, violent, pornographic, or hate-related content; Misinformation and content promoting harm; Personally identifiable information (PII)". "Alibaba distinguishes between functional and safety-focused annotations".
### 02 Model Training Phase - Post-Training
"Our post-training pipeline typically includes techniques such as Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). During these phases, we systematically analyze risks identified through comprehensive evaluations and implement targeted safety enhancements tailored to the nature and severity of each risk."
"Alibaba case study: Producing a High-Quality Safe Corpus ... a safety-aligned corpus does not simply reject problematic inputs. Instead, it provides appropriate, constructive responses: for questions with clear factual answers, it gives accurate and helpful replies; for expressions of distress or negative emotions, it offers empathetic support and positive guidance; and for malicious prompts, manipulative framing, or false premises, it explicitly identifies the issue and avoids falling into logical or ethical traps."
"GRPO safety training strategy: ... Enhanced Reward Model: The reward model is extended beyond traditional dimensions like usefulness, relevance, and conciseness to also evaluate safety and the presence of positive, constructive guidance in responses ... Diversified Risk-Aware Training Data: The GRPO training dataset is deliberately augmented with a wide range of risk scenarios and adversarial prompts".
"Reasoning model ... because this thinking process is exploratory and open-ended, it may generate unsafe content when responding to risky queries. To address this, Alibaba constructs a safe reasoning corpus: the thinking process itself avoids unsafe assumptions or harmful reasoning, and—when faced with malicious instructions—actively analyzes user intent and formulates safe response strategies."
"Multi-lingual and culturally aligned safety ... The Qwen3 models support 119 languages. Alibaba's approach includes two key strategies: Creating universal safety-aligned corpora based on shared human values applicable across all languages; Developing locally adapted safety corpora that reflect the cultural context, religious beliefs, and social norms of each target language region."
### 03 Safety Evaluation Phase - Alibaba Red-Teaming Practice
"Platform setup: Model defense. Alibaba has developed Model Defense, a dedicated attack-and-defense platform for large language models (LLMs). The platform features several model services—including QwenChat, Qwen-VL, and others—as target environments, each equipped with interactive interfaces and vulnerability submission capabilities."
"Red team mobilization: Alibaba convened internal staff with relevant security expertise across the group".
"Vulnerability classification: Discovered vulnerabilities are assessed and prioritized based on multiple criteria, including potential impact, likelihood of successful exploitation, and reproducibility. Through this red-teaming initiative, Alibaba has accumulated a high-quality repository of LLM-specific security vulnerabilities—many of which represent novel attack vectors not previously documented in academic research. Examples include setting-based, encryption-related, and hijacking-style exploits. By analyzing and abstracting these findings, Alibaba internally developed RedChain, a red-teaming tool tailored for LLMs. Built on the LangChain framework, RedChain is an agent-based system that automatically interprets user-defined evaluation goals, generates customized red-teaming test cases at scale, executes evaluations, and delivers structured results".
(Concordia AI 2025, p.59, independently describes the same "Moyu" attack-defense platform and "RedChain" from an Alibaba/CESI Chinese-language report, April 2025.)
### 04 Content-Generation Phase - Model Level
"Guiding the model with security guidelines embedded in the system prompt. By embedding structured, executable safety policies directly into the system prompt, the model is provided with consistent and persistent behavioral constraints."
"Enabling a safety-aware reflection mode during inference ... Starting from Qwen 3, our recent models employ a 'think-before-generate' mechanism when its Thinking Mode is activated ... To defend against adversarial behaviors such as jailbreak attacks, the system incorporates multi-dimensional safety reflection during inference."
(Wan image-generation: prompt rewriting, negative prompts.)
### 04 Content-Generation Phase - Service Level (Alibaba Cloud Model Studio)
"Standard Q&A repository ... When a user's query matches an entry in the repository, the system delivers the pre-approved standard answer directly". "Multimodal risk detection capability: Alibaba's content safety engine provides comprehensive coverage across all modalities (text, image, video, audio) and all risk categories, enabling real-time protection for both model inputs and outputs—supporting both streaming and non-streaming generation modes." Text: classification, semantic similarity, keyword matching; image/video: classification, OCR, image comparison, "recognition of specific public figures"; audio: fingerprinting, ASR.

## Use Guidance - "Prohibited Uses Of Qwen" (verbatim)
"Qwen is intended for lawful, constructive purposes and must not be used to cause harm. It cannot be used to exploit or endanger people, including children, or to promote violence, hate, discrimination, or abuse. Nor may it be used to invade privacy, engage in fraud or deception, spread misinformation, or bypass security safeguards."
Prohibited: "Harm to minors: Creating, distributing, or promoting child sexual abuse material (CSAM) or content that exploits or harms minors. Privacy violations: ... unauthorized data collection, surveillance, or biometric identification (e.g., facial recognition) without explicit consent ... Harmful content: Promoting violence, terrorism, hateful behavior, self-harm, or graphic violence. Fraud and misrepresentation ... Exploitation: Facilitating human trafficking, labor exploitation, or abusive practices. Emotion inference restrictions: Inferring the emotions of a natural person in the areas of workplace and education institutions, unless such use is permitted by applicable laws and regulations."
High-risk: "For high-risk decisions (e.g., healthcare, legal, financial), a qualified human professional must review AI outputs". Healthcare, Legal and financial advice, Critical infrastructure ("Do not use our services to interfere with critical systems (e.g., power grids, healthcare databases, voting machines)"), Law enforcement ("predictive policing, or surveillance unless explicitly permitted").
"Protections For Minors: Qwen may not be used to target, groom, or exploit minors. It is strictly forbidden to generate content that sexualizes minors. Content moderation systems detect and block unsafe prompts and outputs, including harmful or inappropriate content involving minors. Photorealistic depictions of children are restricted".
"How Our Policy Is Enforced: Qwen model rules are enforced through a combination of automated checks, human review, and reports from users. Safeguards are regularly updated to reduce harmful or unsafe outputs. If rules are broken, the Qwen model may investigate, ask for corrections, issue warnings, remove content, limit accounts, or take legal action. For serious or repeated violations, access to Qwen model services may be suspended or ended."
Note: the policy is written for Qwen *services*; nothing in it binds downstream users of Apache-2.0 open weights (Apache 2.0 has no use restrictions).

## "Why Open Weight?" page (verbatim, the lab's only public statement of its open-weight release reasoning)
"To support this, many of our models are released under the Apache License 2.0."
"Openness Builds Trust: Public access to model parameters, documentation, and evaluation tools enables broad inspection and independent verification ... This visibility helps identify vulnerabilities early and improves safety, reliability, and alignment with human values."
"Balancing Openness And Safety: Our most capable models are made available only through our proprietary AI tools or API via Alibaba Cloud Model Studio. This is done with the aim to strike a balance between model safety and the technological benefits stemming from public access to these models. Providing access through a managed API allows real-time monitoring, rule enforcement, rapid response to emerging threats, and continuous safety updates without affecting legitimate users. Maintaining proprietary control over our most advanced models supports this balanced approach."
(Note the tension with the Aug 2026 open release of Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B, a "Qwen-Max-class model".)

## "Our Approach" page
"Alibaba published its first open weight models, Qwen and Qwen Chat, in August 2023." "supporting 201 languages via the Qwen3.5 model family". "Clear usage standards, technical safeguards, and human oversight are part of how the system is developed and managed. The goal is to support decision-making, not replace it. In higher-risk or regulated settings, responsibility remains with the organizations using the system."
