## Why it is in the Qwen3-era KB
Qwen2.5 is the previous generation (0.5B/1.5B/3B/7B/14B/32B/72B). It matters here because (a) its reward-model section is the only place the Qwen tech-report series reports a safety-category score, (b) Qwen2.5-72B-Base/Instruct are used inside the Qwen3Guard data pipeline, and (c) third-party evaluations (Shanghai AI Lab 2507.16534, AgentHazard, multilingual-jailbreak paper) still test Qwen2.5 models alongside Qwen3.

## Safety-related passages (verbatim)

1. Post-training overview (Sec. 4, p.5): "(2) Two-stage Reinforcement Learning: ... Offline RL: This stage focuses on developing capabilities that are challenging for the reward model to evaluate, such as reasoning, factuality, and instruction-following ... Online RL: The Online RL phase leverages the reward model's ability to detect nuances in output quality, including truthfulness, helpfulness, conciseness, relevance, harmlessness and debiasing. It enables the model to generate responses that are precise, coherent, and well-structured while maintaining safety and readability."

2. Reward-model labeling criteria (Sec. 4.3 Online Reinforcement Learning, pp.6-7): "Those criteria ensure that the responses generated by the model are not only high-quality but also aligned with ethical and user-centric standards (Wang et al., 2024a). The specific guidelines for data labeling are as follows:
   - Truthfulness: Responses must be grounded in factual accuracy ...
   - Helpfulness: ...
   - Conciseness: ...
   - Relevance: ...
   - Harmlessness: The model must prioritize user safety by avoiding any content that could lead to illegal, immoral, or harmful behavior. It should promote ethical conduct and responsible communication at all times.
   - Debiasing: The model should produce responses that are free from bias, including but not limited to gender, race, nationality, and politics. It should treat all topics equally and fairly, adhering to widely accepted moral and ethical standards."

3. Reward-model evaluation (Sec. 5.2.3 Reward Model, Table 15, p.15) - the only numeric safety-category result in the Qwen2.5/Qwen3 tech reports:
   "Table 15: Performance comparison across multiple RM benchmarks." Columns: Nemotron-4-340B-Reward | Llama-3.1-Nemotron-70B-Reward | Athene-RM-70B | Qwen2.5-RM-72B.
   - Reward Bench: Chat 95.80 | 97.50 | 98.32 | 97.21; Chat Hard 87.10 | 85.70 | 70.61 | 78.73; **Safety 91.50 | 95.10 | 92.10 | 92.71**; Reasoning 93.60 | 98.10 | 92.19 | 97.65; Score 92.00 | 94.10 | 88.32 | 91.59.
   - RMB: Helpfulness (BoN) 48.85 | 61.02 | 67.24 | 65.72; Helpfulness (Pairwise) 68.70 | 75.28 | 80.82 | 78.83; **Harmlessness (BoN) 50.92 | 52.00 | 67.02 | 56.35; Harmlessness (Pairwise) 70.84 | 69.96 | 80.83 | 73.94**; Overall 59.83 | 64.57 | 73.98 | 68.71.
   - PPE and an internal "Human-Preference-Chinese" accuracy (Qwen2.5-RM-72B 61.27) are also reported.
   Note: these are reward-model scores (the RM's ability to rank safe vs unsafe responses), not policy-model safety scores. Qwen2.5-RM-72B is not open-weight.

## Not present
No policy-model safety benchmark, no red-teaming, no jailbreak, no dangerous-capability, no bias/fairness evaluation of the chat models, no limitations section on safety. Multilingual evaluation (Sec. 5.2.2) is capability-only (IFEval, MMLU-like, MGSM8K extended, BLEnD cultural nuance).
