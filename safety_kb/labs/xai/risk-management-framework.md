# xAI Risk Management Framework (RMF), v1.0

- URL: https://data.x.ai/2025-08-20-xai-risk-management-framework.pdf
- Fetched: 2026-10-19 (raw text: `safety_kb/sources/xai/2025-08-20-xai-risk-management-framework.txt`)
- Document version/date: "Last updated: August 20, 2025" (9 pp.). METR's tracker labels it "Risk Management Framework v1.0". Superseded by the Frontier AI Framework (Dec 2025 / June 2026) but still the document Grok 4, Grok 4 Fast, Grok Code Fast 1 and Grok 4.1 cards cite.

## Scope and structure (p.1-2)
"This RMF discusses two major categories of AI risk—malicious use and loss of control—and outlines the quantitative thresholds, metrics, and procedures that xAI may utilize". Behaviors are bucketed into "abuse potential (e.g., vulnerability to jailbreaks), concerning propensities (e.g., a propensity for deceiving the user), and dual-use capabilities (e.g., offensive cyber capabilities)" — the same three sections every 2025 Grok model card uses.
Sections: Malicious use (benchmarking, risk assessment, safeguards) / Loss of control (same three) / Operational & societal risks (transparency & third-party review, public understanding, information security, responsibility for risks, deployment decisions).

## Malicious use
- Catastrophic threshold definition: "requests that pose a foreseeable and non-trivial risk of more than one hundred deaths or over $1 billion in damages from weapons of mass destruction or cyberterrorist attacks on critical infrastructure ('catastrophic malicious use events')" (p.3).
- Named benchmarks (p.3-4): VCT, WMDP (Bio/Cyber), BioLP-bench, Cybench ("40 professional-level Capture the Flag (CTF) challenges").
- Bio/chem threat model (p.4-5): ideation/design/build/test cycle [Nelson & Rose 2023]; five restricted "critical steps" — Planning, Circumvention (supply-chain controls), Materials (Select Agents / Australia Group / CWC Schedule I), Theory, Methods (serial passage, viral rescue, BSL-3/4 work in unapproved facilities, genetic manipulation, pathogenicity quantification). "These steps were identified in close collaboration with domain matter experts at SecureBio, NIST, RAND, and EBRC." Enforcement = "AI-powered filters that specifically monitor user conversations for content matching these narrow topics".
- Radiological/nuclear: "Assessments to date lead xAI to conclude that its models do not substantially increase the likelihood of malicious use of nuclear and radiological materials" — no evaluation described (p.5).
- Cyber: "Independent third-party assessments of xAI's current models on realistic offensive cyber tasks requiring identifying and chaining many exploits in sequence indicate that xAI's models remain below the offensive cyber abilities of a human professional. Thus, xAI currently relies on our basic refusal policy" (p.5). Third party not named.
- **Threshold (verbatim, p.5):** "We developed an internal benchmark of benign and restricted biology and chemistry related queries in collaboration with SecureBio. Our risk acceptance criteria for system deployment is maintaining an answer rate of less than 1 out of 20 on restricted queries. We plan to add additional thresholds tied to other benchmarks."
- Safeguards: safety training, system prompts, input/output filters (p.5-6). Note the admission that refusal is "incidental alignment resulting from post-training (our models naturally tend to refuse malicious requests even without any safety-specific training data)".

## Loss of control
- Benchmarks: MASK (honesty under pressure) and Anthropic's answer-sycophancy setting (p.6-7).
- **Threshold (verbatim, p.7):** "Our risk acceptance criteria for system deployment is maintaining a dishonesty rate of less than 1 out of 2 on MASK."
- Risk assessment relies on real-world observation: "xAI has assessed its models' propensities in real-world settings and the models do not exhibit high levels of concerning propensities", plus public deployment on X as a monitoring channel.
- Safeguards: honesty training, instruction-hierarchy training, system prompt instruction not to deceive.

## Operational / societal (p.7-9)
- Transparency items xAI "may publish": RMF adherence reviews (with anonymous internal reporting and anti-retaliation), benchmark results on major releases, "Internal AI usage: Assess the percent of code or percent of pull requests at xAI generated by our models", employee surveys.
- Information security: "appropriate information security standards sufficient to prevent its critical model information from being stolen by a motivated non-state actor"; measures "against the large-scale extraction and distillation of reasoning traces". No standard, audit or threat-model tiering named.
- Incident response: may notify law enforcement, revoke accounts, "temporarily fully shut down the relevant system", post-mortem. Whistleblower protection to government agencies.
- Deployment: tiered availability; explicitly allows releasing a model "that poses a high risk of some forms of malicious cyber use ... to certain trusted parties if it would empower defenders more than attackers".

## What is NOT run or disclosed
- No capability thresholds for dual-use knowledge (VCT/WMDP/BioLP/Cybench are measured but have no trigger); only two numeric criteria exist (filter answer rate <5%, MASK dishonesty <50%).
- No radiological/nuclear evaluation; no autonomy / AI R&D / self-replication threat model; no persuasion threshold.
- No uplift studies; no description of how safeguards are red-teamed; no named third-party evaluators; no security standard or audit; no governance body or board oversight; "deployment" not defined as internal vs external.
- No commitment language ("may", "plan to") for most measures.

## External criticism (see `external-criticism.md`)
AI Lab Watch (Zach Stein-Perlman, 2025-09-08): "Overall the RMF is profoundly unserious"; MASK <50% "very silly"; security claim "not credible". SaferAI (arXiv 2512.01166): xAI overall 16%, risk treatment 5% (lowest of 12), but relatively high on risk modelling (31%) for its explicit bio causal pathways.
