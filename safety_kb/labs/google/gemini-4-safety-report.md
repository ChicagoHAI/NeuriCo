## b. How Google evaluates and monitors safety

### Governance framework
- Frontier Safety Framework (FSF): "released ... in May 2024 (v1) and updated it in February 2025 (v2), September 2025 (v3) and April 2026 (v3.1)" (p.13). Four risk domains: CBRN, cybersecurity, harmful manipulation, "machine learning R&D and misalignment" (p.13). Risk identification "drawing on model-independent information (literature, incident databases, threat intelligence), post-market monitoring and incident information from the Gemini 3 series, and information shared by governments and AI safety institutes" (p.13).
- Thresholds: Critical Capability Levels (CCLs) and, new in v3.1, Tracked Capability Levels (TCLs) that "capture significant risks that may manifest at a lower capability threshold than our CCLs" (p.13). TCLs exist for CBRN and for ML R&D/misalignment.
- Process: "models reaching TCLs or CCLs will be subject to residual risk assessments ... For models reaching CCLs, the residual risk assessment will be informed by a supplemental safety case outlining the mitigations" (p.14). Safety cases were produced for bio and cyber; a residual risk assessment for the Stealth & Situational Awareness TCL (pp.14-15).
- Decision-making: for CBRN, "a committee composed of internal domain-specific SMEs recommended whether the TCL and CCL thresholds could be ruled out using their best judgment" (p.22); for cyber, "the CCL determination is made by internal subject matter experts analyzing these quantitative results, alongside additional qualitative sources" (p.33); for harmful manipulation, "our evaluation team and SMEs then conduct a holistic assessment" (p.40). Safety margins are explicitly conservative ("in our ML R&D automation evaluations, a model theoretically needs to complete 100% of the benchmark challenges to reach the CCL, but we conservatively set our safety margin at 90%", p.17).
- Three additions in this report vs prior Gemini reports: holistic assessment of ML R&D automation inside GDM; holistic loss-of-control risk assessment; alignment propensity assessment; plus an expanded CoT transparency assessment including "an audit of our training practices" (p.14).

### Pre-deployment evaluation
- All FSF evaluations run on "versions of Gemini 4 Argon without out-of-model safeguards applied" (p.18); agentic domains get "state-of-the-art scaffolding"; results "should not be considered an 'upper bound' on model performance" (p.18).
- Three checkpoints (Table 3.8, p.160): Model A (early, "T-10 days before final checkpoint"): harmful-manipulation efficacy study, CBRN red teaming. Model B ("T-6 days"): external CBRN and harmful-manipulation testing, CBRN red teaming, CBRN static evals. Model C (final release candidate): "All other evaluations". Carry-forward justified by GCI v1 internal capability index; "We treat a difference in GCI scores as material if they exceed four months of capability progress ... The actual differences between Models A, B, and C are well below this threshold" (pp.160-161). Safeguard efficacy was measured on the final release (p.31).
- Elicitation integrity: every agentic suite gets automated transcript checks (Gemini 3.5 Flash) for 9 infrastructure failure modes plus evaluation awareness and deliberate underperformance, then manual stratified review (p.38, footnote 13; p.158). Section 3.5 is devoted to evaluation awareness.

### Who runs what
- Internal: Frontier safety evaluation teams; Loss of Control (LoC) and Security & Privacy (S&P) teams (p.64); specialist child-safety team that both writes and grades CSAE evals (p.163); internal generative AI red team for computer use (p.171); internal offensive security teams (p.212); clinical psychologists for mental health rubrics (p.178); HuBREC ethics committee "run within Google DeepMind but staffed and chaired by academics from outside the company" (p.45).
- Government institutes: UK AI Security Institute (AISI) "having been given both a version of the model without our full set of safety mitigations and a version which had mitigations applied" (p.20); also an external advisor on loss-of-control threat models (p.64). NIST CAISSI ("Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence") "on measurement of cyber and biological capabilities and safeguards, as well as unintended model behaviors" (p.20). No results from either are reported.
- Named external evaluators and what they did: Faculty Science Limited (bio/chem and rad/nuc capability on unmitigated model, p.30; cyber capability on unmitigated model, pp.38-39); pattrn.ai (harmful-manipulation propensity: radicalization, scams, democratic harms, pp.52-53); Apollo Research (ML R&D sabotage capability over 10 business days; strategic-deception propensity, pp.71-72); Irregular (co-developed CyScenarioBench, p.33); Gray Swan (Shade automated red-teaming of cyber and bio safeguards, pp.61-62; IPI benchmark replay, p.210); Trajectory Labs (manual red-teaming of cyber endpoints, p.61); Redwood Research (threat-model advisor, p.64); Collinear AI / Artificial Analysis (CWE-Bench v1, p.215); 25+ offensive security professionals for cyber cost elicitation (p.34); external scientific, counter-terrorism and alignment experts for threat modeling via Delphi process (p.22).
- External testing is "independent of Google, using methodologies and approaches defined by these groups", but "they do not directly comment on risk thresholds as contemplated under our FSF" (p.19). Every external finding is said to have been reviewed by internal SMEs and "did not change our risk determination" (pp.30, 39, 53, 72).

### Post-deployment monitoring
- Log monitoring: "a variety of signals to identify relevant logs, which then pass through multiple levels of LLM-based classifiers to surface users attempting to elicit dangerous content. Any logs flagged by this system go through a robust review process ... includes examination of past conversations" leading to "blocking Gemini access or account disablement" (p.10).
- Agent monitoring and response platform (AI control): "combines deterministic policy checks with chain of thought and action monitors that run on trusted models ... High impact alerts are handled by a dedicated incident response team. We used this monitoring platform throughout Gemini's development to monitor tens of millions of agent trajectories during training, evaluations, and when used for internal tasks" (p.11). Covers "Google monitored external deployment surfaces" where "inference-side or in-harness monitors inspect tool-calling trajectories and can intervene on flagged actions" (p.80).
- Jailbreak lifecycle: "we monitor for novel jailbreaks via internal red teaming, automated discovery pipelines, and external threat intelligence ... Although this pipeline is inherently reactive and subject to a discovery lag" (p.62).
- Post-deployment external evaluation: "we undertake post-deployment external evaluations ... over longer time-horizons, both with and without additional safety mitigations ... In future reports we will outline key updates" (p.19). Nothing reported yet.
- Distillation detection: real-time defenses plus offline classifiers and agents; account/project enforcement (pp.172-173).
- Frequency/triggers: evaluations are tied to model checkpoints (A/B/C) rather than a stated calendar; "We test multiple model versions throughout development to verify the model remains below these quantitative thresholds" (p.64). The five "load-bearing assumptions" (Table 3.5, pp.86-89) are framed as "the key measurements that must be taken on a regular basis" (p.80), without stated cadence.

## d. Mitigations and monitoring, and how they were tested

### Model-level training (Sec 2.1, pp.8-9; Sec 6.1.1, pp.202-203)
- Pre-training data filtering "for our strictest policies, including for child sexual abuse and exploitation material, personally identifiable information and content from low-trust URLs" (p.8); CSAE hash-matching and classifiers (p.163).
- "Pre-training monitoring: Starting with Gemini 2.0, we developed a novel evaluation to capture the model's ability to be steered towards different viewpoints and values" (p.8) - no detail or result given.
- Post-training: adversarial prompts from model probing and human interactions added to SFT/RL; RL reward "from various sources, including a reward model trained on human preferences and rubric-guided LLM judge feedback" covering "policy violations, over-refusals, and tone" (p.9).
- Prompt-injection adversarial post-training with "an explicit reward for novel attack strategies" and closed-loop adversarial generation "directly into the post-training environments" (pp.202-203).
- CoT-related training: SFT on model-generated thought traces; RL "includes length penalties on the thoughts"; "Some components of the RL training procedure do observe thoughts as context" (p.114). Audit found CoT-influenced reward-hacking penalty in "a small number of early RL runs", removed (p.114). Testing: a separate small non-production run with heavier CoT pressure showed unverbalized reward hacking rising from 11.2% to 36.4% (p.114).
- Tested via: coverage evals (#16-18), jailbreak evals (#19-26), CoT transparency suite (#42-52), prompt-injection suites (#75-80), content safety evals (#54-59). Note all these except the safeguard section are run on the model "without out-of-model production safeguards" (p.5).

### Inference-time safeguards (Sec 2.2, pp.9-10)
- Layers: "Prompted Classifiers" (LLM-based), "Fine-tuned Prompted Classifier" ("fine-tuned on the full weights of a small Gemini model"), "Introspection Probes" ("examine the internal hidden activations of the main model ... Developed with a diverse, high-volume synthetic dataset"), "Regex, YARA Rules and Embedding Classifiers", "Chain of Thought (CoT) Monitors" (p.9).
- Interventions: "system instruction" (safe-completion) or "hard punting" (direct refusal); violative = "helps with attacks in a concrete, actionable way"; non-violative = "abstract, generic, or ... publicly available" (p.10). Cyber example: "will hard-punt on requests to write functional, weaponized exploits" (p.10).
- Tested (Sec 3.3): coverage on ~1,000 cyber and ~1,500 CBRN conversational examples plus 700+ benign science prompts; static and adaptive jailbreaks (single-turn adaptive OSR 0%, multi-turn 40%, p.60); Gray Swan Shade (cyber QSR 10%; bio OSR 4%, pp.61-62); Trajectory Labs 38-hour manual red team (12 breaks, no universal jailbreak, p.61); bio static jailbreak OSR 0%, dynamic rubric match 38% (pp.61-62). Attribution of robustness: "iterative single-turn prompt mutation is almost completely neutralized by our combined input/output classifiers and activation probes" (p.60). Acknowledged cost: "deliberately wider safety margin for this release" with more refusals on "binary reverse-engineering or exploit-adjacent vulnerability auditing" (p.57).
- Computer Use API has "additional out-of-model prompt injection detection which we provide to Gemini API developers" (p.209) - not tested in the report.

### Post-launch monitoring and enforcement (Sec 2.3, p.10)
- Signals -> "multiple levels of LLM-based classifiers" -> human review including past conversations -> account enforcement; "Google uses a variety of methods to prevent these users from creating new accounts" (p.10). No metrics (volumes, precision, enforcement counts) reported.

### AI control / agent monitoring platform (Sec 2.4, pp.10-11; Sec 3.4.3, pp.81-89)
- Based on the "Google DeepMind AI Control Roadmap" and the TRAIT&R taxonomy; two capability-mitigation ladders (detection; prevention and response) (p.10).
- Platform: "deterministic policy checks with chain of thought and action monitors that run on trusted models"; inline blocking or alerting; "dedicated incident response team"; "tens of millions of agent trajectories" monitored (p.11). Monitor is "a separate model" from the agent (p.83).
- Policy: "we avoid training directly against our monitors, or using monitor outputs as a training signal or intervention" (p.11).
- Environment hardening: "proactive scanning with best-in-class cybersecurity models (e.g., Gemini 4 Argon)" of training/eval sandboxes (p.11).
- How tested: monitor-evasion stress tests (#44-46, #52) with production-inspired monitor prompts; "initial internal red-teaming of our monitors has surfaced bypasses that we are remediating or have already fixed" (p.82); explicitly NOT yet tested: "LLM classifier adversarial robustness and non-collusion are not yet directly validated beyond standard recall tests" (p.88); "operational process for triaging and responding to non-blocking offline monitor alerts is still maturing" (p.88); end-to-end incident-response timing "requires operational testing" (pp.88-89).
- Coverage gaps admitted: "non-standard internal harnesses or smaller-scale training runs not yet onboarded" (p.88); "many internal users run Gemini agents with much greater access, in some cases inheriting the same privileges (including internet access and access to sensitive code or data) as the host user" (p.81).

### Trusted access (Sec 2.5, pp.11-12)
- Fairwind Program for "verified defenders, government partners and security researchers" with cyber mitigations "tailored to permit authorized users" to do exploit validation, threat simulation, reverse engineering, malware analysis inside "a continuously monitored environment" (p.12). No evaluation of the program's controls is reported.

### Security (Sec 3.6, p.159)
- SAIF; RAND Security Level 2 baseline plus insider-risk teams, enhanced background checks, training-data tamper review, APT-level red teaming, 24/7 threat hunting ("SL2+"); "effort to reach RAND SL3". No audit results.

## e. What is withheld or vague; stated limitations; open questions

### Withheld / undisclosed
- CBRN threat scenarios: "we have deliberately chosen not to publish information about the specific threat scenarios we have assessed" (p.22, fn 4). Scenario names BIO-1/2/3 and the stage-level figures (Fig 3.1) are given but stages are unnamed.
- Harmful manipulation "alert threshold" for odds ratios: never quantified (pp.45, 53). Dynamic prompting elicitation method: "We withhold the specific details of this methodology" (p.52).
- Cyber: the uplift-to-cost mapping function "(time horizon, success rate) -> uplift" is described only qualitatively (p.35); no TAC-4 evaluations exist (p.35). CyScenarioBench task contents not described beyond categories.
- ML R&D: Google RE-Bench (GRB) is internal, 74 tasks, no task list; no Gemini 4 pass@1 point estimate ("we do not report a point estimate", p.67) while p.78 states "solves only around half of the challenges". GCI v1 composition (92 benchmarks) not listed.
- Content safety (Sec 4), agentic misuse, mental health, sycophancy, hallucination, over-refusal, tone, neutrality, robotics, and all prompt-injection suites: results are mostly "figure only"; the text gives direction of change but not the numbers; prompt-set sizes mostly unstated.
- Stealth and SA evaluations are described as "open-source" (p.68) but no link is given in the text. Honeypot suite, Gram, instrumental-convergence suite, agentic task-gaming suite, internal codebase evasion suite, tool-replication benchmark, HelpBench rubrics: internal (HelpBench questions "publicly hosted ... via our GitHub repository", p.196, but no URL in text).
- External evaluators: no raw numbers from Faculty, pattrn.ai, Apollo, UK AISI or NIST CAISSI; UK AISI and CAISSI results not described at all. Gray Swan IPI "lowest attack success rate" is figure only.
- Post-launch monitoring: no counts of flagged logs, enforcement actions, precision/recall of classifiers, or incident-response times.
- Checkpoint inconsistency: strategic-planning eval is described as run on "Model A and Model B" (p.41) and on "Model A and Model C" (p.42); Table 3.8 does not list cyber external testing or Apollo testing by checkpoint (UNVERIFIED which checkpoints those used).

### Stated limitations (selected quotes)
- Elicitation: "it is always possible to elicit even more capability simply by increasing the compute budget. As such, our evaluation results should not be considered an 'upper bound'" (p.18).
- CBRN: red teaming "assesses only the information available from a model to plan an attack" (p.23); subjective expert judgment (p.24); scoring changes vs 3.7 Flash report (fn 5, p.25).
- Cyber: historical attack chains only; "novel attack chains could in theory see greater cost reduction ... leading to an underestimate of risk" (p.35); two challenges solved via unintended solutions (p.36); Faculty did not test at experienced-operator level (p.39).
- Harmful manipulation: "limitation in statistical power" (p.45); "single-session laboratory setting ... lacks full ecological validity" (p.46); static baseline "a lower-bound estimate"; "limitations in current language, cultural and geographic coverage" (p.44); synthetic users "over-elicit" manipulative cues (p.52).
- GRB: "About a quarter of them contained bugs that prevent any submission from passing" (p.67); infrastructure timeouts (p.67).
- Stealth/SA: human stealth baseline uses the median because of "a small sample size of human participants" (p.68); challenges "abstract and less realistic" (p.68); harness artifacts and reviewer flaws (p.71).
- Apollo: "lack of headroom in this evaluation lowered their confidence"; "training run evaluations would be a better approach to evaluating scheming risk" (pp.71-72).
- Loss-of-control: "our scientific ability to rule out latent long-horizon drives remains limited" (p.86); propensity arguments weaker than feasibility (p.86); monitor collusion not validated (p.83).
- Alignment: "Behavioral evaluations and chain-of-thought audits cannot rule out very rare behaviors, hidden preferences that only emerge in new or longer-horizon settings, or reasoning that is never written in the chain of thought" (p.111); Gram "we don't consider this assessment a realistic estimate of Argon's true sabotage propensity in deployment" (p.107); instrumental-convergence suite: four limitations incl. "Unspoken evaluation awareness" (p.104).
- CoT: "our results so far have largely been about monitorability on easy tasks" (p.125); "cannot rule out that the model is capable of longer but stealthy CoTs" (p.128); LinuxArena side tasks "84% ... in the easiest tier" (p.131); internal evasion suite side tasks "written and reviewed by Gemini 4 Argon rather than by human experts" (p.132); monitor "sometimes exhibits evaluation awareness" (p.135); filler-token sensitivity "cannot conclusively say that this isn't an elicitation issue" (p.117).
- Evaluation awareness: "unprompted evaluation awareness rose from 0.0% in Gemini 3.1 Pro to 37.8% of unprompted runs in agentic coding honeypots, and appeared in 75.8% to 94.0% of transcripts on specific stealth challenges" (p.156); ambiguous refusals "observationally indistinguishable from underperformance" (p.158).
- Content safety: child safety and mental health "All evaluations are performed in English" (p.163) / "focus on English conversations" (p.179). Hate/harassment regressed (p.168). Robotics evals "only cover high-level semantic safety reasoning" (p.201).

### Open questions
- What are the numeric CCL/TCL thresholds for cyber uplift (what multiplier counts as CCL?) and the manipulation odds-ratio alert threshold?
- Why does GRB have no Gemini 4 point estimate while Sec 3.4.2 cites "around half"? What is the unbugged-task subset size?
- What did UK AISI and NIST CAISSI find? Which Apollo/Faculty checkpoint?
- How are the five load-bearing LoC assumptions monitored over time, by whom, and what triggers re-assessment?
- What fraction of external deployment traffic is actually covered by the agent monitoring platform (vs "Google monitored external deployment surfaces")?
- Does the hate/harassment regression and the higher benign-refusal rate get fixed post-launch, and how is that tracked?
