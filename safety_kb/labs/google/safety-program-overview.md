# Google DeepMind: how safety is evaluated and monitored across the model lifecycle

Fetched: 2026-10-19. Sources (all local under sources/google/): FSF v3.1 (fsf_v3-1.txt), Gemini 3 Pro FSF Report Nov 2025 (gemini_3_pro_fsf_report.txt, "G3P"), Gemini 3.7 Flash FSF Report Aug 2026 (gemini_3-7_flash_fsf_report.txt, "G37F"), Gemini 3 Pro model card (model-card-gemini-3-pro.txt), Gemini 3.7 Flash model card (model-card-gemini-3-7-flash.md), "An Approach to Technical AGI Safety and Security" (arXiv 2504.01849, "AGI paper"), Responsibility & Safety page, Gemini API abuse-monitoring docs (gemini-api-abuse-monitoring.md), GTIG "Adversarial Misuse of Generative AI" (google-cloud-adversarial-misuse-gen-ai.md), blogs on double-blind evals, bioresilience, harmful manipulation, cyber evaluation framework. Gemini 4 Argon specifics are in gemini-4-safety-report-notes.md (other agent) and only cross-referenced here.

## 1. Lifecycle view

### 1.1 Pre-training / training
- Model cards list training-phase mitigations: "dataset filtering; conditional pre-training; supervised fine-tuning; reinforcement learning from human and critic feedback; safety policies and desiderata; product-level mitigations such as safety filtering" (Gemini 3 Pro model card p.8-9).
- "Training/Development Evaluations including automated and human evaluations carried out continuously throughout and after the model's training, to monitor its progress and performance" (Gemini 3 Pro model card p.7).
- FSF: material capability change assessments "typically conducted upon the completion of new post-training runs on various checkpoints" (FSF v3.1 Glossary); G3P: capability evals "may be triggered ... upon the completion of a pre-training or post-training run, on various candidates of a model version" (p.3).
- CoT-monitorability-aware training: "we use supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning on thought traces ... The reinforcement learning stage includes length penalties on the thoughts ... With some minor exceptions, other rewards are computed without reference to the thoughts" (G3P p.18). Gemini 4 report adds an audit of training practices and a disclosed lapse (see gemini-4-safety-report-notes.md, Sec 2.4 fn 1).
- Training-time monitoring for eval-awareness: "we also briefly investigated model outputs from post-training for signs of evaluation awareness ... manually, with an anomaly detection autorater, and an eval awareness autorater ... RL training transcripts seem particularly fruitful to study" (G3P p.25).
- Safety fine-tuning for frontier domains: "SFT to teach models to recognize relevant risk areas ... RLHF to further improve training robustness ... a new framework, called consequence-aware risk analysis" (G37F p.28).

### 1.2 Post-training / pre-deployment
- Standard model-card evaluation types (Gemini 3 Pro model card p.7): development evals; "Human Red Teaming conducted by specialist teams who sit outside of the model development team"; "Automated Red Teaming to dynamically evaluate Gemini for safety and security considerations at scale"; "Ethics & Safety Reviews ... ahead of the model's release"; plus FSF testing.
- Content-safety: automated evals for child safety, hate speech, dangerous content, harassment, sexually explicit, medical advice, etc. (model cards); human red teaming must satisfy "required launch thresholds" for child safety (both model cards).
- FSF critical capability assessment before first external deployment (see frontier-safety-framework.md). Early warning evals are run with "no out-of-model safeguards applied" (G37F pp.9, 13, 25) and "state-of-the-art scaffolding and affordances" for agentic domains (G37F p.5); for ML R&D G37F used "a scoped version of the same internal agent scaffolding that Google researchers use" (p.37).
- Elicitation: "the most effective intervention for eliciting stronger performance has been increasing test-time compute ... more attempts per task, allowing the model to think for longer, and extending the number of steps" (G37F p.38). The AGI paper acknowledges "it remains an open question how much elicitation effort is appropriate" (Sec 5.2.3). Fine-tuning-based elicitation is not mentioned in either FSF report.
- Which checkpoint is tested: G3P external and internal CBRN/misalignment evals were run on "a similar earlier version" of the model; the final version got "limited additional testing" after a material-capability-change check (G3P pp.5-6, 16). Gemini 4 Argon: two checkpoints (Models B, C) for external testing.
- Safeguard evaluation before launch (G37F pp.29-33): three axes — Coverage (Violation Rate, Benign Refusal Rate on expert-authored + synthetic + "sanitized log mining" sets: ~1,000 cyber conversational examples 1-5 turns; ~1,500 CBRN conversational; 700+ benign "CBRN Science" examples), Adversarial Robustness (static template jailbreaks; adaptive automated attacker; "Chem/Bio Dynamic Jailbreak Eval" multi-conversation aggregation against rubrics; Gray Swan "Shade" agent on 28 cyber and 53 bio tasks; reported as Objective Success Rate vs Query Success Rate), Impact on Benign Uses ("random traffic that we assume to be benign"). Result claims: "~0% violations for topics related to bio weapons queries"; key-skills benchmark with mitigations: "The model refuses on 100% of the milestones."
- Safety case: required only once a CCL is reached; residual risk assessment otherwise (FSF 1.3.4). G37F's misalignment section self-critiques: "a relatively concentrated safety case relying on a simple argument and a small amount of highly legible evidence" (p.41).
- Governance sign-off: "The decision to proceed with launch was reviewed and approved through Google's internal decision making processes" (G3P p.22). CBRN decisions: "a committee composed of internal CBRN stakeholders recommended whether the TCL and CCL thresholds could be ruled out ... and communicated this decision to leadership" (G37F p.7).

### 1.3 Deployment-time safeguards (what "monitoring" concretely means)
Layers, as described in G37F pp.28-29 (and nearly identically in the Gemini 4 report Sec 2.2-2.3):
1. In-model training (SFT/RLHF).
2. Online guardrails / classifiers:
   - "Prompted Classifiers. These LLM-based classifiers take in user conversations and output labels regarding malicious intent. Developed using AlphaEvolve, they are continuously updated".
   - "Introspection Probes: This classifier examines the internal hidden activations of the main model to detect latent representations of requests and responses for harmful content. Developed with a diverse, high-volume synthetic dataset ... difficult for black-box attackers to evade."
   - "Regex, YARA Rules and Embedding Classifiers ... near-instant reaction times for newly escalated threats."
   - Prompt-injection defenses (G3P p.21): "prompt injection content classifiers, security through reinforcement, markdown sanitation and suspicious URL redaction, user confirmations, and end-user security mitigation notifications".
3. Interventions: "either a 'system instruction' (safe-completion) or 'hard punting' (direct refusal) depending on the classifier labels" (G37F p.29). Violative = "helps with attacks in a concrete, actionable way"; non-violative = "abstract, generic, or ... publicly available" (G3P p.21; G37F p.29). Cyber example: will analyze known public exploits, "will hard-punt on requests to write functional, weaponized exploits."
4. Misuse detection and account actions (G37F p.29): "The proactive misuse detection system uses a variety of signals to identify relevant logs, which then pass through multiple levels of LLM-based classifiers to surface users attempting to elicit dangerous content. Any logs flagged by this system go through a robust review process to determine true positive malicious intent. This process includes examination of past conversations as well as signals that indicate collusion with other violative accounts. Violations can lead to ... blocking Gemini access or account disablement."
5. Jailbreak lifecycle (G37F p.33): "we monitor for novel jailbreaks via internal red teaming, automated discovery pipelines, and external threat intelligence. Jailbreaks that we assess as particularly risky are escalated for mitigation (e.g., blocklists, filter updates, or model fine-tuning) ... Although this pipeline is inherently reactive and subject to a discovery lag, this feedback loop aims to reduce the effective lifespan of jailbreaks."
6. Developer-facing abuse monitoring (Gemini API docs, last updated 2026-06-09): "Google's Trust and Safety Team employs a combination of automated and manual processes"; automated scans for Prohibited Use Policy violations ("hate speech, harassment, sexually explicit content, and dangerous content"); manual review if "a project consistently exhibits suspicious activity"; prompts, context and outputs retained 55 days "solely for the purpose of policy enforcement"; graduated responses: email outreach, rate limits / model switching ("changing which model answers a specific request"), temporary suspension, account closure, with appeal link.
7. Threat-intelligence monitoring: Google Threat Intelligence Group analyses state-backed actor use of the Gemini app ("mix of analyst review and LLM-assisted analysis"); Jan 2025 report found APT/IO actors (Iranian heaviest) using Gemini for reconnaissance, scripting, content/persona generation; "We did not observe any original or persistent attempts by threat actors to use prompt attacks" (google-cloud-adversarial-misuse-gen-ai.md). The cyber eval framework blog says Google "analyzed over 12,000 real-world attempts to use AI in cyberattacks in 20 countries" (blog-cyber-threats.md).
8. Agentic / internal monitoring (FSF: "input/output/chain-of-thought monitoring and analysis" as a deployment mitigation, Glossary p.19; "chain-of-thought monitoring on high-risk deployments" for Stealth/SA TCL). The Gemini 4 report describes an agent monitoring and response platform with CoT and action monitors and an incident response team (see gemini-4-safety-report-notes.md Sec 2.4) — this is the first concrete description of misalignment-oriented production monitoring; the FSF reports for 3 Pro and 3.7 Flash do not describe any.
9. Model-weight security monitoring: SAIF; SL2+ "proactive threat hunting with 24/7 incident response capabilities"; "security infrastructure at Google is subject to penetration testing" (FSF 1.3.4). Mitigations listed in G3P p.21: "identity and access management, physical security, red teaming, endpoint security, infrastructure hardening, vulnerability reward program, threat detection and response."

The AGI paper (Sec 5.4) frames misuse monitoring design space: inputs (prompt, hidden first response, prior interactions, cross-user patterns, KYC background), techniques (activation probes, custom classifier LMs, prompted LMs, manual human labelling), responses (terminate, insert refusal tokens, "switch to a weaker model ... to gather more data on harmful use patterns"), and cost/latency/false-positive trade-offs. It argues monitors should be combined because of "decorrelated failure modes".

### 1.4 Post-deployment
- FSF: "post-market monitoring, including information about incidents relating to our frontier safety risk domains" can update residual risk assessments and safety cases; material updates go back to the governance function (FSF 2.1.2 step 3).
- Incident response: "we strive to learn from our post-market monitoring mechanisms, including as part of our detection, mitigation, response and/or reporting of incidents relating to our frontier safety risk domains" (FSF 1.3.2). No incident taxonomy, SLA, or public incident reporting is described.
- Red-team cadence: described only as "continually" ("Internal and external red team efforts continually test the efficacy of the mitigations", G3P p.21; G37F p.31). No numeric cadence.
- Post-deployment external evaluation: introduced in the Gemini 4 report ("we undertake post-deployment external evaluations ... over longer time-horizons, both with and without additional safety mitigations ... In future reports we will outline key updates", Sec 3.1.3). Not present in G3P/G37F.
- Bug bounties / vulnerability reward program listed as deployment mitigation (FSF Glossary; G3P p.21).
- Bioresilience programme (blog 2026-07-16): "a four-step safety process: threat modeling, evaluations, mitigations and monitoring"; "more than 15 partnerships with government bodies, biosecurity organizations, and research groups"; adapting SynthID watermarking to DNA synthesis screening.

## 3. Third-party / external evaluators: what Google says
Standard language (G3P p.5; G37F p.6; Gemini 4 Sec 3.1.3, near-verbatim across reports):
- "We work with a set of specialist independent groups to help assess and identify areas for improvement in our model safety by undertaking structured evaluations, qualitative probing and unstructured red teaming. We choose these organizations based on their domain expertise".
- "This testing is independent of Google, using methodologies and approaches defined by these groups."
- "The independent external evaluators explore frontier safety risk domains, however they do not directly comment on risk thresholds as contemplated under our FSF. They have their own taxonomies of harm ... Our subject matter experts review, translate, and integrate these results into our own internal risk analyses" (G37F p.6).
- Findings are checked for consistency with internal evals and "informed the CCL determination" (G3P p.5).

What they were asked to test (G3P pp.7-17):
- CBRN: "scenario based red teaming exercise" (chem/bio); R/N: evaluator rated "moderate malign misuse potential (on a scale of minimal, moderate, significant, to catastrophic)".
- Cyber: "operationally relevant scenarios ... loosely aligned with taxonomies such as the MITRE ATT&CK and Unified Kill Chain", with human baselines.
- Harmful manipulation: "approximately 75k prompts" on radicalisation/extremism and democratic harms, scored by "the testers' own SME-validated classification model".
- ML R&D: "potential for catastrophic harm via sabotaging AI research" during autonomous ML R&D deployment.
- Misalignment: "propensity to covertly pursue misaligned goals or take deceptive actions ... Strategic deception ... Red teaming scenarios mimicking internal AI R&D". Finding: "Gemini 3 Pro exhibits a substantial propensity for strategic deception in certain limited circumstances" (G3P p.17) — judged non-severe because of low stealth/SA capability.

Access and timing:
- G3P: external testing "was performed on a similar earlier version to the final version" (p.5). G37F: only "additional light-weight external safety testing" because Flash-class models are not the usual safety-buffer carrier; "We perform external safety testing on model versions across the Gemini 3 model family" (p.6).
- Mitigations on/off: G3P/G37F do not say whether external testers had the unsafeguarded model. Gemini 4 report: evaluators got "a version of Gemini 4 Argon without the full set of our safety mitigations, where relevant and appropriate"; UK AISI got both mitigated and unmitigated versions; NIST CAISSI measured cyber/bio capabilities and safeguards (Sec 3.1.4). Fine-tuning access, weights access, and raw CoT access are not mentioned in G3P/G37F; the Gemini 4 report mentions "raw chain-of-thought traces" as a form of proportionate access.
- Named organisations across the fetched corpus: UK AISI (advisor on threat models; evaluator for Gemini 4), Redwood Research and Apollo (advisors), Gray Swan and Trajectory Labs (safeguard red teaming), Panoplia Laboratories (wet-lab RCT, via Frontier Model Forum), Singapore AISI / OpenMined / AVERI / MLCommons (double-blind evaluation pilot, Aug 2026), NIST CAISSI (Gemini 4). The G3P/G37F reports anonymise their "third party evaluator(s)".
- Double-blind evaluation pilot (blog 2026-08-27): Confidential Space enclave so that "The evaluator cannot see the Gemini model weights, and Google cannot see the evaluator's test prompts"; piloted on "a Gemini Flash Lite model"; "we don't rely on internal testing alone ... specialized research labs, civil society and national AI Safety and Security Institutes (AISIs)". No results published in the blog.
- Secondary sources (UNVERIFIED, from web search only): commentary that UK AISI received Gemini 2.5 Pro access only after public release (LessWrong/EA Forum posts). Not confirmed from Google documents fetched here.

## 4. Published artefacts and disclosure practice
- Per-model: model card (content-safety evals + FSF summary table) and, for Pro-class frontier releases, a standalone FSF report (Gemini 3 Pro; Gemini 3.7 Flash got one "precautionarily"; Gemini 4 Argon got a 217-page safety report).
- Not disclosed: alert-threshold numeric values (all domains), CBRN scenarios, key-skills challenge contents, GRB tasks, manipulation steering instructions, identities of most external evaluators, external evaluators' raw reports, incident statistics, monitoring precision/recall or false-positive rates.
