# G42 — Frontier AI Safety Framework

- URL: https://www.g42.ai/application/files/9517/3882/2182/G42_Frontier_Safety_Framework_Publication_Version.pdf (raw `safety_kb/sources/other-frameworks/g42-frontier-safety-framework.txt`)
- Fetched: 2026-10-19. February 2025 (METR: Feb 6 2025), 12 pp. "developed with input from SaferAI and METR". Authors: Andrew Jackson, Boulbaba Ben Amor, Adele O Herlihy, Larry Murray, Rohit Manucha, Grzegorz Kosior, James Wilton.

## Frontier Capability Thresholds (initial list)
- Biological threats: "Enabling an individual with only introductory biology experience in developing a biological weapon, through providing detailed advice, automating biological processes, etc. Or, facilitating the design of novel biological weapons with substantially greater potential for damage." Preliminary evals: open-source benchmarks "(e.g., LAB-Bench, WMDP-Bio)" and benchmarking bio-chem models; in-depth: expert-relative Q&A, "evaluate human participants' ability to conduct realistic but safe wet lab experiments with and without language model assistance"; notes biological design tools (BDTs). Required: DML 2 / SML 2.
- Offensive cybersecurity: "Automating powerful cyber offensive operations against unsecured or secured targets, in a way that could cause critical damage." Preliminary: "Cybench, eyeballvul"; in-depth: exploit secured targets in safe environment. DML 2 / SML 2.
- Future candidates: Autonomous Operation, Advanced Manipulation.
- Evaluation logic: "If a given G42 model achieves lower performance on relevant open-source benchmarks than a model produced by an outside organization that has been evaluated to be definitively below the capability threshold, then such G42 model will be presumed to be below the capability threshold." In-depth evals use elicitation (prompting, fine-tuning, agentic tools), testing in Arabic/Hindi where relevant; reliance on upstream evals when fine-tuning open models. Capability reports "at least once every six months" to the Frontier AI Governance Board and Executive Leadership Committee.

## Deployment Mitigation Levels (DML 1-4)
DML1 model cards, incident channels, security training; DML2 real-time input/output classifiers, robustness testing, asynchronous monitoring, controlled rollout — objective "Even a determined actor should not be able to reliably elicit CBRN weapons advice or ... automate powerful cyberattacks"; DML3 anomaly detection, adversarial simulation/red teaming with SMEs — "resist sophisticated attempts to jailbreak"; DML4 "To be defined" — resist state-supported attempts.

## Security Mitigation Levels (SML 1-4)
SML1 none ("G42 may choose to open source models"); SML2 RBAC, geofenced access, network segmentation, internal+external red teaming — "highly unlikely that a malicious individual or organization (state sponsored, organized crime, terrorist, etc.) could obtain the model weights"; SML3 multi-party/quorum approval, end-to-end weight encryption, key rotation, zero-trust — resist "concerted attempts, with support from state programs"; SML4 to be defined.
Rule: "If a necessary Deployment Mitigation Level cannot be achieved, then the model's deployment must be restricted; if a necessary Security Mitigation Level cannot be achieved, then further capabilities development of the model must be paused."

## Governance
Frontier AI Governance Board (Chief Responsible AI Officer, Head of Responsible AI, Head of Technology Risk, General Counsel); anonymous reporting; "Annual Governance Audits ... independent internal audits"; "annual external audits to verify compliance"; "annual transparency report"; detailed information shared with "the UAE Government"; phased implementation plan (0-6, 6-12, 12+ months).

## What is NOT disclosed / not yet done
No evaluation results or transparency report were found (none linked from the framework; not searched further — UNVERIFIED whether the promised annual transparency report exists); DML4/SML4 undefined; no numeric benchmark thresholds; autonomy and manipulation not yet tracked; SaferAI (which helped write it) scores G42 third overall at 24% (governance 38%, highest among non-big-three).
