## Structure (2026 revision)
1 Critical Risk Domains (each with a Critical Capability Threshold); 2 Evaluating Frontier Models for Critical Risks; 3 Risk Mitigations (Abuse / Security / Misalignment safeguards); 4 Governing the Framework; Appendix A: Amazon's foundational security practices.

## Critical Risk Domains and thresholds (verbatim, p.2-3)
- **CBRN Weapons Proliferation**: "AI at this level will be capable of providing expert-level, interactive instruction that provides material uplift (beyond other publicly available models in known harnesses) that would enable a non-subject matter expert to reliably produce and deploy a CBRN weapon."
- **Offensive Cyber Operations**: "AI at this level will be able to enable non-subject matter experts to discover novel end-to-end exploit chains in hardened systems or utilize existing vulnerabilities to autonomously extend control over critical infrastructure systems."
- **Harmful Manipulation** (new in 2026): "capable of providing material uplift (beyond other publicly available models in known harnesses) that would enable a malicious actor to systematically and substantially distort the beliefs or behavior of large populations or high-stakes decision-makers through multi-turn interactions with the model in ways that could reasonably be expected to cause severe harm." Flagged as "an emerging area of research and evaluation".
- **Loss of Control** (new in 2026, replaces "Automated AI R&D"): "capable of autonomously planning and executing complex sequences of expert-level tasks (including, but not limited to, the research, development, and deployment of frontier models) in such a way that would impair the ability to direct, modify, or shut down the model." Evaluations "will include rigorous measurement of a model's propensity for deception, autonomous goal-directed planning, and the ability to circumvent safeguards within an agentic scaffolding".
- Feb 2025 version (per Nova Premier paper, arXiv 2507.06260 §5): the third domain was "Automated AI R&D", defined as the point at which an AI system can "replace human researchers and fully automate the research, development, and deployment of frontier models that will pose severe risk—such as accelerating the development of enhanced CBRN weapons and offensive cybersecurity methods."
- "Uplift" is measured relative to "other publicly available models in known harnesses" — a relative, moving baseline.

## Evaluation process (p.3-4)
"We conduct internal and, as appropriate, external evaluations on an ongoing basis, including during training and prior to deployment ... We will re-evaluate deployed models prior to any major updates". "Maximal capability evaluations" early in development, pre-deployment evaluations of mitigations. "When an evaluation indicates that a model's capabilities meet a Critical Capability Threshold, we will not deploy the model until safeguards appropriately mitigate the risks." Methods: (a) automated benchmarks (public + proprietary expert-built), (b) expert red teaming (vendors + in-house, "specialized firms and academics"), (c) uplift studies ("controlled trials that compare the abilities of a group with access to the new frontier model to ... a group without"), with and without agentic scaffolding; plus "lighter-touch automated benchmark assessments on a recurring basis, including after model launch". No compute trigger, no cadence beyond "ongoing", no named benchmarks in the framework itself (see model-card notes for WMDP, ProtocolQA, BioLP-Bench, VCT, Cybench, RE-Bench, etc.).

## Mitigations (p.4-6)
Abuse safeguards: training-data review/redaction; alignment training (SFT, LHF/LAIF, "RL algorithms and agentic gyms"); runtime input/output moderation (incl. prompt-injection/jailbreak detection); fine-tuning safeguards ("resilient to malicious customer fine-tuning"); access controls/suspension; abuse-detection classifiers ("automated and does not involve human review of user inputs"); incident detection & response ("We report incidents to relevant authorities as appropriate"); post-production monitoring incl. bug bounty — "In November 2025, Amazon launched a private AI bug bounty program to strengthen Amazon Nova models."
Security safeguards: Nitro-based confidential compute, VPC isolation, AES-256-GCM with FIPS 140-3 L3 KMS, hardware-token MFA for Critical Permission Groups, 24/7 SOCs, threat intel (MadPot/Sonaris/Mithra); Appendix A covers insider-threat monitoring ("Workforce vetting and careful monitoring for insider threats"), separation of duties, zero-operator-access services, formal methods, "Third party audits and red teaming assessments of AI systems ... These audits validate that our model weights are not exposed".
Misalignment safeguards (new): persona-based alignment training, "exposing the model to ethical actions ... during pre-training/mid-training", fine-tuning guardrails against increased misalignment propensity, post-production monitoring.
Stop rule: "In the event evaluations reveal that an Amazon frontier model meets or exceeds a Critical Capability Threshold and our Risk Mitigations are unable to appropriately control the capabilities ... we will not deploy the model until we have identified and implemented additional safeguards as appropriate."

## Governance (p.6-7)
Incorporated into the "Amazon-wide Responsible AI Governance Program"; updates reviewed by "the SVP for the model development team, the Chief Security Officer, and legal counsel"; threshold-exceeding evaluations reported to those two leaders who make the "go/no-go decision"; "Amazon will publish, in connection with the launch of a frontier AI model, information about the frontier model evaluation for safety and security"; revisit "at least annually and publish any material modifications". No board role, no independent review body, no whistleblower channel described, no changelog.

## Third-party testing
Framework text names only METR/FMF (feedback on framework). Evaluation papers name Nemesys Insights (CBRN) and METR (AI R&D) as "independent auditors" who "reviewed results on test sets, scoring rubrics, and safety-judge notes" (i.e., review of Amazon-run evals, not independent runs; for Nova 2 Lite Nemesys ran a ~800-participant study). Service cards cite an "Independent Red Teaming Network" (ActiveFence, Innodata, Deloitte/Gryphon, Gomes Group at CMU, Nemesys). No AISI/CAISI engagement stated.

## What is NOT disclosed / not run
- No security levels tied to thresholds (unlike RAND SL-style tiers); no quantitative thresholds or pass/fail benchmark criteria; no cadence; no compute trigger.
- No definition of what "known harnesses" or "publicly available models" baseline means in practice.
- Harmful manipulation and loss of control have no evaluation methodology published yet; prior "Automated AI R&D" domain quietly removed/renamed without a changelog.
- The Feb 2025 PDF is no longer accessible at its URL; no versioned archive.
- External ratings: SaferAI (arXiv 2512.01166, assessing the 2025 text) overall 18%, risk identification 11%, analysis 16%, treatment 23%, governance 21%. METR lists Amazon among the 12 publishers.
