# Microsoft Responsible AI Transparency Report (2025 and 2026 editions)

- 2026 edition is "our third annual Responsible AI Transparency Report" covering roughly July 2025–June 2026.

## 2025 report — key safety/evaluation facts
- AI Red Team (AIRT): "a centralized team of professional red teamers that operates independently of product teams"; "In 2024, AIRT conducted 67 total operations across Copilots and models, including every flagship model added to the Azure OpenAI Service and every Phi model released"; new modalities red-teamed (image-to-image, video-to-text, text-to-video, text-to-audio); "up-skilling 1,500 internal and external cybersecurity and AI professionals"; integrated third-party tools "InspectAI and Vivaria, for cybersecurity exercises"; PyRIT "grown to over 2,500 stars on GitHub"; April 2025 "PyRIT's integration with Azure AI Foundry" (AI Red Teaming Agent).
- Phi program ("Measure and manage"): break-fix cycle; "an average of 75% reduction in the amount of harmful content generated by Phi 3 models"; XSTest used "to measure both appropriate and inappropriate refusal rates"; Phi-3.5 multilingual gap ("the model sometimes generated refusals in English, even when the request ... was in another language"); Phi-4 "significant safety gains ... robustness to jailbreak techniques and higher refusal rates"; Phi-4-Multimodal audio: model "inferred sensitive attributes in 27% of test prompts ... a system safety message ... dropped this rate down to 0.4%".
- Governance: Sensitive Uses and Emerging Technologies program; Responsible AI Standard; Frontier Governance Framework introduced (Feb 2025) and described as integrating with pre-deployment review.
- Not disclosed: operation-level findings, any capability-threshold classification, incident counts for models.

## 2026 report — key safety/evaluation facts
- Scale: "more than 450 reviews of Sensitive Uses and nearly 2,500 generative AI product and feature reviews from July 2025 through June 2026"; "formalizing 13 incident response playbooks" (partial quote, multi-column).
- AIRT: "our AI Red Team testing increased 1.5x, executing more than 100 operations"; new attack-vector work on "adversarial memory poisoning" and "techniques to detect backdoors in open-weight LLMs"; measurement emphasis on "psychosocial, offensive cyber, and insufficient robustness, reliability, or accuracy"; "increased coverage for evaluations of agentic systems".
- External testing: "Microsoft has put in place a Memorandum of Understanding with the US Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) and a Collaboration Agreement with the UK AI Security Institute (AISI)" so that "qualified external evaluators with national security expertise can help examine how our AI models and systems behave under challenging and unexpected conditions"; also partnerships "with US CAISI, UK AISI, and institutes in Australia and Singapore"; "External Red Team Alliance (EXTRA)" with "18 universities across six continents"; contributions to MLCommons benchmarks.
- Evaluation science: STAC framework for measurement validity (flags "significant validity gaps in commonly used red teaming metrics and in LLM-as-judge evaluations"); 2026 "Adaptive Spec-driven Scoring for Evaluation and Regression Testing (ASSERT)" open-source framework; "RAMPART" for agent security claims; onboarding of additional open-source benchmarks to an internal platform.
- Frontier Governance Framework: re-engineered Responsible AI Standard with "scenario requirements" for "frontier models, agentic AI, or sensitive interactions"; "In early 2026, Microsoft updated our Frontier Governance Framework ... The update clarifies how Microsoft tracks high-risk capabilities such as harmful manipulation and loss of control, and it introduces new disclosures about our ongoing red teaming and risk modeling practices. While the changes do not reflect shifts in operational policy, they provide greater transparency into how Microsoft evaluates and monitors frontier model risks, engages third-party evaluators, and supports internal reporting and accountability."
- Case study MAI-Image-1 / Image-2: five-step layered evaluation (raw model without guardrails -> text/image filters -> internal adversarial datasets -> external benchmark platform -> child-safety review; threshold tightened so children are generated less often); Image-2 "manual review of more than 2,000 generated images"; "all sexualized or exploitative content involving children was blocked"; MAI built "a scalable, product-specific safety evaluation platform".
- Case study MAI-Thinking-1: risks in focus "harmful content, agentic risks, and cyber misuse risks"; released via "private preview with a select group of customers, followed by broader testing and public preview release in August 2026".
- Emerging Technologies (EmTech) program launched 2025 to "proactively examine frontier AI capabilities—such as memory and Computer Use Agents".

## What is NOT disclosed (both editions)
No FGF leading-indicator or deeper-assessment results for any model; no risk classification published; no CAISI/AISI findings; no incident statistics; no jailbreak/ASR numbers for Copilot products; no CBRN/cyber capability numbers; no security-audit results. The reports describe process volume (operations, reviews) rather than outcomes.
