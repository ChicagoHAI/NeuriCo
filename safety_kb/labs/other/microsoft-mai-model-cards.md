## MAI-DS-R1 (DeepSeek-R1 post-trained; HF card, spring 2025)
- URL: https://huggingface.co/microsoft/MAI-DS-R1 (raw `mai-ds-r1-readme.txt`); base DeepSeek-R1 671B; MIT.
- Purpose: "post-trained by the Microsoft AI team to improve its responsiveness on blocked topics and its risk profile"; training "110k Safety and Non-Compliance related samples from Tulu 3 SFT dataset" + "approximately 350k multilingual examples of internally developed topics" (quoted in summary). Objective: "Post-trained to reduce CCP-aligned restrictions and enhance harm protection".
- Evals: public benchmarks (accuracy/pass@1); "Blocking Test Set: 3.3k prompts on various blocked topics from R1, covering 11 languages" — unblocked 99.3% (R1-1776 same, higher "Satisfaction" 0-4); "Harm Mitigation Test Set: a split from the HarmBench dataset" scored by attack success rate per functional/semantic category — "MAI-DS-R1 outperforms both R1-1776 and the original R1 model in minimizing harmful content". Tables are images; no numeric ASR in text.
- Not disclosed: HarmBench ASR numbers, Toxigen/other benchmarks (not present), red teaming, any FGF assessment. Card warns the model "may output politically sensitive content".

## MAI-Thinking-1 (35B active / 1T total MoE; technical report + card, public preview Aug 2026)
- URL: https://microsoft.ai/pdf/mai-thinking-1.pdf ("MAI-Thinking-1: Building a Hill-Climbing Machine", raw `mai-thinking-1-model-card.txt`, ~110 pp.). "trained from-scratch, exclusively on clean, enterprise-grade data, without distillation from third-party models"; 8K GB200 pre-training, 4.6K GB300 RL.
- §3.4.3 Safety training: taxonomy of harmful vs borderline prompts; harmful prompts from vendor/internal red-teaming and automated attacks ("Template-based attacks, e.g., PyRIT", "PAP", "TAP"); borderline "do-not-refuse slice". Reward = safety judges on policy compliance, response engagement (penalizes over-refusal and compliance on severe requests), response style; "safety-gated aggregator" (non-compliant rollout gets minimum reward). Audit note: "87.8% of policy-non-compliant responses received reward model scores ≥ 3" — i.e., the general reward model alone would reward unsafe outputs.
- §4.3 Internal safety evaluation: safety pass rate (judge score >3 of 5) vs helpfulness (1 - over-refusal) by eight harm categories vs Claude Sonnet 4.6 — "Across five of the eight categories, MAI-Thinking-1 sits above and/or to the right ... largest gains on CBRN, Self Harm, and Elections & Politics" (figure only). Jailbreak suite: "2.5K unique seed scenarios from vendors, internal red-teaming, and open-source benchmarks including HarmBench and StrongREJECT ... final evaluation set of approximately 9.5K jailbreak prompts"; ASR (Fig. 21, with provider-side filters for others): Foundational — MAI-Thinking-1 4.4%, GPT-5.4 3.0%, Opus 4.6 7.0%, Sonnet 4.6 5.7%; Compositional — 17.6% / 13.9% / 17.4% / 15.0%; Adaptive (TAP, multi-turn) — 32.3% / 26.8% / 25.1% / 26.4%.
- §5.1 Internal red teaming: "fifteen engagements ... over 2,170 goal-based adversarial scenarios across 25 policy categories", 5-10 turns each; six recurring attack patterns (multi-turn escalation under benign pretext, fictional framing, credentialed persona, "gradual recursion or formatting drift", in-context age-indicator bypass, authoritative-document fabrication); "aggregate attack success fell by roughly 22% from pre-mitigation to the final candidate. We reduced jailbreak success by ∼44%, hate & fairness by ∼43%, child safety issues by ∼30% and mental health attacks by ∼20%". Scope limits: "testing was primarily in English ... agentic tool-use and multimodal inputs were out of scope".
- §5.2 Independent red teaming: "Microsoft's AI Red Team (AIRT) and third-party vendors" (unnamed) on automated attacks, code/cyber misuse, psychosocial harms, multilingual coverage. **"Structured dangerous-capability / uplift evaluations (Phuong et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024b) are out of scope for this release."** Findings: TAP adaptive attacks were a gap (closed-loop remediation pipeline built); low-resource-language framing (Yoruba, Telugu, Amharic, Burmese, Khmer, Malay) elicited refused content — partly mitigated.
- Appendix J: two-stage LLM-judge evaluation (request classification on harm category, sensitivity, intent, scenario, jailbreak detection -> response specification); prompt pool from public benchmarks, PII-filtered consumer Copilot logs, vendor prompts; informativeness-weighted sampling (tinyBenchmarks-style IRT); release gating: "Every internal release candidate must pass an acceptance threshold on a fixed bundle of safety evaluations and must do so without regressing against the previous accepted release", with thresholds "placed at a fixed percentile along" a safety/over-refusal/quality Pareto frontier "rather than at a static absolute number".
- Not disclosed: absolute safety pass rates, over-refusal rates, the acceptance thresholds, vendor names, any FGF leading-indicator/deeper-assessment result or risk classification, CBRN/cyber/autonomy capability numbers (explicitly out of scope), CAISI/AISI testing.

## MAI-Code-1-Flash (137B/5B-active MoE, June 2, 2026)
- URL: https://microsoft.ai/pdf/MAI-Code-1-Flash-Model-Card.PDF (raw `mai-code-1-flash-model-card.txt`). Depends on MAI-Thinking-1 mid-training checkpoint; RL on ">150,000 environments"; GitHub Copilot in VS Code only.
- Safety evaluations: "a combination of cybersecurity benchmarks and secure coding assessments, including CyberBench, CyberSecEval, and SecRepo, to assess its ability to withstand real-world security threats, avoid introducing vulnerabilities, and align with secure coding standards. Additional safety evaluations were conducted through a structured release process using production model APIs with safety classifiers and filters applied." No numbers. Capability table: SWE-Bench Verified 71.6, SWE-Bench Pro 51.2, Terminal Bench 2 54.8, AIME 2026 92.5, HLE 18.
- Not disclosed: any safety score, red teaming, offensive-cyber capability assessment, FGF status.

## MAI-Image-2 (Mar 18, 2026), MAI-Image-2.5 (Jun 2, 2026), MAI-Image-2.5-Pro (Jul 23, 2026), MAI-Image-2.6 / 2.6-Flash (Aug 14 / Sep 4, 2026)
- URLs: https://microsoft.ai/pdf/MAI-Image-2-Model-Card.pdf, .../MAI-Image-2.5-Model-Card.PDF, .../MAI-Image-2.5_Pro-Model_Card.pdf, .../MAI-Image-2.6-Model-Card.pdf (raw `mai-image-2-model-card-guess.txt`, `mai-image-2.5-model-card.txt`, `mai-image-2.5-pro-model-card.txt`, `mai-image-2.6-model-card.txt`). 20B non-embedding params (2.5/2.6); "10B-50B" (2).
- Alignment: "reduce the generation of harmful or inappropriate (e.g., violent, gory, sexual) images, even when requested by a user"; defense-in-depth (data filtering + system-level prompt/output classifiers).
- Safety evaluation and red teaming (Image-2): "The Microsoft AI Red Team (AIRT) conducted multiple rounds of red teaming ... attack strategies spanning eight harm categories ... pre-mitigation and post-mitigation ... break-fix cycle ... Residual findings generally required moderate to high levels of adversarial effort to reproduce." Image-2.5/2.5-Pro repeat this ("multiple harm categories"); Image-2.6: "evaluated across a range of adversarial safety scenarios, including higher-severity content. Results indicate safety performance broadly consistent with the prior model version." Elo tables for quality only (Image-2.5 overall 1254 vs Image-2 1190 vs Image-1 1093).
- Not disclosed: any quantitative safety metric (block rates, ASR, CSAM/NCII test results), harm-category list, external red teaming; cards say "will be updated as training completes and evaluation data becomes available". The 2026 RAI Transparency Report case study gives more process detail (2,000-image manual review for Image-2; child-safety threshold tightening).

## MAI-Voice-2.1 (Oct 1, 2026)
- URL: https://microsoft.ai/pdf/MAI-Voice-2.1-Model-Card-Memo.pdf (raw `mai-voice-2.1-model-card-memo.txt`). TTS, 23 languages, instant voice cloning from 5-60 s audio. Safeguards: cloning is gated ("Access requires Microsoft approval"; Azure Custom Neural Voice Limited Access review; recorded consent statement required). No evaluation of deepfake/impersonation misuse, watermarking, or speaker-verification bypass disclosed.

## Cross-cutting gaps
No MAI card publishes an FGF risk classification, leading-indicator result, CBRN/cyber capability score, or third-party evaluator name, despite the FGF's commitment to share "the model's risk classification" publicly. The 2026 RAI report states CAISI/AISI agreements exist but no MAI model card references them.
