# Microsoft Frontier Governance Framework (FGF)

- Fetched: 2026-10-19 (raw: `safety_kb/sources/microsoft/frontier-governance-framework-feb-2025.txt`, `frontier-governance-framework-feb-2026.txt`)
- Versions: "Version 1 (February 2025)" (14 pp., change log "8 February 2025 – First version"); "February 2026" (15 pp.), change log: "One year update following finalization of the EU Code of Practice for General Purpose AI (CoP), NY RAISE Act and CA Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act including: Adding harmful manipulation and loss of control as tracked high-risk capabilities; Adjusting the update cadence to align with regulatory obligations; Aligning the scope our framework to that of regulations; Adjusting the cadence by which we repeat deeper capability assessment to align with emerging industry standards; Adding a definition of substantially fine-tuned models that are in scope".

## Tracked high-risk capabilities
2025: CBRN weapons; Offensive cyberoperations; Advanced autonomy ("complete expert-level tasks autonomously, including AI research and development"). 2026 adds: Loss of control ("undermine effective human control through adaptive, deceptive, or self-reinforcing mechanisms such that ... a model can no longer be reliably directed, modified, or shut down") and Harmful manipulation ("strategically distort human behavior or beliefs at scale"). 2026 adds a risk-modeling process per capability ("prioritize specific scenarios, threat actors ... technical bottlenecks").

## Scope trigger — the main 2025 -> 2026 change
- 2025: leading-indicator assessment runs on "any model that teams at Microsoft are optimizing for frontier capabilities or that Microsoft otherwise expects may have frontier capabilities" and "any model pre-trained using more than 10^26 FLOPs".
- 2026: "any model that is in scope for frontier model requirements under applicable laws, such as the EU AI Act, California's Transparency in Frontier AI Act (TFAIA), and New York's Responsible AI Safety and Education (RAISE) Act. We also run a leading indicator assessment when Microsoft substantially fine-tunes first- or third-party models, where the compute used for fine-tuning is more than 1/3 of the base model" (default "1/3 of 10^25 FLOPs" when base compute unknown). The explicit 10^26 trigger is gone; scope is now whatever regulation defines.

## Leading-indicator assessment (both versions)
Benchmarks for six precursor capabilities: general reasoning; scientific & mathematical reasoning; long-context reasoning; spatial understanding; autonomy, planning and tool use; advanced software engineering. Inclusion criteria (fn.1): "1) have low saturation (i.e., the best performing models typically score lower than 70%); 2) measure an advanced capability ... 3) have a sufficient number of prompts". Run "during pre-training, after pre-training is complete, [2026 adds: after post-training,] and prior to deployment", and "at least every six months". "Frontier capabilities" = "a significant jump in performance beyond the existing capability frontier in one advanced general-purpose capability or beyond frontier performance across the majority". Microsoft states the intent to "move this leading indicator screening toward a more direct assessment of tracked capabilities" as evals mature — i.e., currently it does NOT directly test CBRN/cyber at the screening stage. No benchmark names published.

## Deeper capability assessment
Capability evaluation (adversarial testing, documented with "a statement on the robustness of the evaluation method"), capability elicitation ("fine-tuning the model ... multi-agent setup, leveraging prompt optimization, or connecting the model to whichever tools and plugins will maximize its performance"; "Resources applied to elicitation should be extrapolated out to those available to actors in threat models"), holistic risk assessment (system-level mitigations, marginal uplift "over and above ... currently available open-weights models"). 2026 adds third-party language: "We engage qualified third parties to conduct evaluations in ways that are appropriate to the risk profile of the model; sometimes this requires granting API access ... and sometimes it requires a review of testing conducted internally. The decision to involve qualified and expert external actors ... is made by internal experts who are independent from the model development team. Where applicable, we disclose the extent to which and how qualified third parties are involved." Cadence: 2025 "at least once every six months"; 2026 only "if there are material changes to the deployed model's risk profile".
Pre-mitigation score low/medium/high/critical per capability; high/critical => "further review and safety and security mitigations prior to deployment".

## Capability thresholds (Appendix I; identical in both years for the first three)
- CBRN: Low = no response or public info; Medium = "some assistance increasing the ability of an existing expert (PhD level ...) to develop a known CBRN threat"; High = "meaningful uplift to an expert's ... ability to develop and deliver a novel CBRN threat" or "to a medium-skilled actor's (e.g., STEM education) ability to develop and deliver a known CBRN threat"; Critical = "meaningful uplift to an expert's ability to develop a highly dangerous novel threat or significantly lowers the barriers to a low-skilled actor developing and delivering a known CBRN threat".
- Offensive cyber: Low = gathers public info, "cannot meaningfully plan or execute a cyberattack"; Medium = "increases the ability of a low-skilled actor to conduct low-level spoofing, phishing, or social engineering attacks by partially automating discrete tasks ... (but not execution)"; High = "meaningful uplift to a low-to-medium skilled actor's ability to create and conduct highly disruptive or destructive cyber-attacks, including on critical infrastructure, for example, through discovering novel zero-day exploit chains or developing complex malware"; Critical = "meaningful uplift to a low-skilled actor's ability to identify and exploit major vulnerabilities or enables a well-resourced and expert actor to develop and execute novel and effective strategies against hardened targets".
- Advanced autonomy: Low = "basic tasks ... less than one hour"; Medium = "a few hours of human labor, but requires human intervention"; High = "multiple days' worth of generalist human labor and appropriately correct for complex error conditions, or autonomously complete the vast majority of coding tasks at the level of expert humans"; Critical = "fully automate the AI R&D pipeline at a fraction of human labor costs".
- Loss of control / Harmful manipulation (2026): no thresholds — "We are studying and progressing methods ... researching approaches to evaluating models ... and setting appropriate risk acceptance criteria".

## Mitigations
Security: baseline for any model triggering leading-indicator assessment; High-risk models get measures "protective against most cybercrime groups and insider threats" (restricted weight access, encrypted weights, "Advanced security red teaming, using third parties where appropriate"); Critical: "high-trust developer environments, such as hardened tamper-resistant workstations with enhanced logging, and physical bandwidth limitations". References NIST SP 800-53, 800-218, SOC 2, RAND "Securing AI Model Weights", FMF practices; 2026 adds "we also reference the RAND security level framework as a benchmark".
Safety: harm refusal; deployment guidance/documentation ("a summary of evaluation results, the deeper capability assessment, and safety and security mitigations"); monitoring & remediation (abuse monitoring; "automated monitoring in chain-of-thought outputs" [2025 text]; 2026: incident response policies, "provide reports to regulatory authorities and impacted customers as appropriate"); phased release / trusted users. Post-mitigation re-evaluation to low/medium; "If ... we identify a risk we cannot sufficiently mitigate, we will pause development and deployment".

## Governance
Executive Officers responsible for the AI governance program make the final deployment decision on a "recommendation for secure and trustworthy deployment"; "independent internal audit and board oversight"; anonymous employee reporting with anti-retaliation; Chief Responsible AI Officer reviews updates. Update cadence: 2025 "Every six months"; 2026 "At least every twelve months ... All material revisions will be made public within 30 days of adoption". Public disclosure of "capabilities and limitations ... relevant evaluations, and the model's risk classification" — none has been published for any MAI model as of 2026-10-19 (see microsoft-mai-model-cards.md).

## What is NOT disclosed
No benchmark names for leading indicators; no quantitative thresholds; no published pre-/post-mitigation scores or risk classifications for any model; no named third-party evaluators in the framework; no security-level mapping to specific controls; loss-of-control and manipulation thresholds absent; the 10^26 compute trigger was removed rather than tightened; deeper-assessment cadence relaxed from 6-monthly to event-driven. External ratings: SaferAI (arXiv 2512.01166) Microsoft overall 18% (risk identification 7%, analysis 13%, treatment 28%, governance 25%); AI Lab Watch (Sep 2025) Microsoft 3% overall.
