## ASF 1.0 (2024) — the frontier-style version
Two risk categories: "loss of control" ("AI systems causing severe disempowerment of the human species") and "misuse" (e.g., "hazardous biochemical weapons"). AI risk assessment scale for loss of control: frontier AI evaluated "Every 3 months, or when performance increases by 6x". Misuse handled by an AI risk assessment matrix (use case x need for guardrails): special-purpose + high need -> "Do not deploy"; special-purpose + risk identified -> authorized users only; general + risk -> withhold until mitigated. Korean safety datasets built by NAVER: SQuARe, KoSBi, KoBBQ. Governance: Future AI Center + Board risk committee. First "Generative AI Red Teaming Challenge" (April 2024, seven domains). No benchmarks or thresholds for CBRN/cyber capability were specified.

## ASF 2.0 (July 2026) — pivot to "service-centered" safety
Motivations: "On-Service AI", multi-model environments, and Korea's AI Basic Act (in force January 22, 2026; "High-Impact AI" special domains). Three pillars: (1) Defining and Classifying Risk — AI Risk Taxonomy of 3 subjects of protection (users, members of society, AI service ecosystem) x 3 protected values (life & physical safety, economic value, preventing unjust discrimination); (2) Identifying and Assessing Risk — AI Impact Assessment Matrix (special vs general domain; broad vs narrow scope) with pre-launch impact assessment for high-impact special-domain services; (3) Managing and Mitigating Risk — safety evaluation ("whether the AI service operates as intended and whether it produces harmful or inaccurate outputs") and user communication (generative-AI labeling). Operational structure CHEC 2.0 (company-wide consultation process). Governance: AI Safety Center (established March 2026 under the Chief Corporate Responsibility Officer), three-layer oversight with Board of Directors (Risk Management Committee) as final decision-maker. External: Korea AISI, TTA, SNU AI Policy Initiative, UN Global Compact; "NAVER AI Safety Progress Report" publications.

## What changed / what is missing
ASF 2.0 drops the explicit loss-of-control / misuse framing, the quarterly / 6x evaluation cadence, and the biochemical-weapon example; it contains no capability thresholds, no named benchmarks, no model-weight security measures, no red-teaming commitments, and no pause conditions. In Seoul-commitment terms it is now a product-governance framework rather than a frontier-risk framework. No evaluation results for HyperCLOVA X are published in either document. SaferAI (scoring the 2024 text) gives Naver 10% overall (7/7/8/18).
